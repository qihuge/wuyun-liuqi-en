# Chapter 12: Case Validation — Practical Application Cases of Five Motions and Six Qi Calculation

## 1. Case Description

### 12.1 Nature and Purpose of Cases

Before entering specific cases, the nature and purpose of the cases in this chapter must first be clarified:

**First, the cases in this chapter are demonstrative cases, not clinical validation evidence.**
- Due to the current lack of large-sample real clinical data, the cases in this chapter are **demonstrative cases** based on Five Motions and Six Qi theory and algorithms, used to demonstrate the complete process and practical application method of calculation.
- The patient information (birth time, symptoms, medical history, etc.) in these cases is constructed for demonstration purposes, not real clinical cases.
- Therefore, the cases in this chapter cannot be used as clinical validation evidence for the accuracy of Five Motions and Six Qi calculation. True clinical validation needs to follow the methodology in Chapter 11, collect large-sample real clinical data, and conduct systematic statistical analysis.

**Second, the purpose of the cases is to demonstrate the application process of the algorithm.**
- Through specific cases, demonstrate how to start from user input (birth date and time), step by step perform stem-branch calculation, Five Motions and Six Qi calculation, Dynamic Stagnation Point calculation, treatment recommendation, Four Diagnostic Methods auxiliary, and finally generate a complete report.
- Allow readers to intuitively understand the practical application method of the Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm, rather than just staying at the theoretical level.
- Provide reference for subsequent clinical validation — future real case validation can be carried out according to the process in this chapter.

**Third, the cases cover different disease types and Five Motions and Six Qi configurations.**
- This chapter selects 5 cases, corresponding to cardiovascular system, digestive system, respiratory system, gynecology, and emotional diseases respectively, covering different Core Organs and disease types.
- At the same time, the birth years of these 5 cases are different, and the configurations of Annual Motion, Sovereign Heaven, Earthly Deputy, etc., are also different, demonstrating the calculation results under different Five Motions and Six Qi configurations.
- Through the comparison of multiple cases, the application scope and flexibility of the Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm can be more comprehensively demonstrated.

### 12.2 Unified Format of Cases

For ease of reading and comparison, all cases in this chapter adopt a unified format:

1. **Case basic information**: Patient number, gender, age, birth date and time, chief complaint, present illness history, past history, etc.
2. **Four Pillars stem-branch**: Year pillar, month pillar, day pillar, hour pillar.
3. **Five Motions and Six Qi calculation**: Annual Motion, Sovereign Heaven, Earthly Deputy, Principal Motion, Guest Motion, Principal Qi, Guest Qi, Guest-Principal Superimposition, special year judgment.
4. **Dynamic Stagnation Point calculation**: Trigger Point, Stagnation Point, Tong Shi substitution, Core Organs, Affected Meridians, Six Qi brake, High-Risk Period, Exacerbation Period, onset risk index.
5. **Treatment recommendation**: Stagnation Point treatment, Trigger Point treatment (brake), Affected Meridians treatment, prevention suggestions, comprehensive treatment plan.
6. **Four Diagnostic Methods auxiliary**: Reference information for inspection, auscultation-olfaction, inquiry, palpation, and auxiliary judgment of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis.
7. **Case discussion**: Discuss the calculation results of this case, analyze its rationality and possible validation directions.

### 12.3 Limitations of Cases

When reading the cases in this chapter, the following limitations need to be noted:

First, **the cases are constructed, not real**. As mentioned above, the cases in this chapter are constructed for demonstration purposes, and the patient's symptoms, medical history, etc., are "reverse-matched" according to the calculation results, rather than real clinical observations. Therefore, the "consistency" between calculation results and symptoms in the cases is artificially constructed and cannot prove the accuracy of calculation.

Second, **the number of cases is limited and cannot represent the population**. This chapter only has 5 cases, the sample size is too small to conduct statistical analysis, and cannot represent the general population. True validation requires hundreds, thousands, or even tens of thousands of large samples.

Third, **algorithm parameters need to be verified**. The various parameters used in the cases (power values, coefficients, thresholds, weights, etc.) are based on theoretical derivation and empirical settings, marked as [to be verified]. Whether these parameters are accurate directly affects the reliability of calculation results.

Fourth, **lack of control group**. The cases only have the results of "Five Motions and Six Qi calculation," without comparison with other methods (such as conventional TCM syndrome differentiation, Western medicine diagnosis), nor comparison with random guessing. Without control, it is impossible to determine whether calculation is truly valuable.

Fifth, **lack of prospective validation**. The calculations in the cases are "retrospective" — after knowing the patient's symptoms, then see whether the calculation results match. Truly persuasive validation is "prospective" — without knowing the patient's symptoms, first perform calculation and prediction, then see whether the prediction matches the actual.

Despite these limitations, the cases in this chapter still have their value — they demonstrate the complete application process of the Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm, provide specific examples for readers to understand the algorithm, and also provide reference and templates for subsequent real clinical validation.
