# Chapter 9: Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis — Combination of Five Motions and Six Qi with Inspection, Auscultation-Olfaction, Inquiry, and Palpation

## 1. Significance of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis

### 9.1 Why Five Motions and Six Qi Calculation Needs to Be Combined with Four Diagnostic Methods

In the previous chapters, we learned how to calculate an individual's congenital endowment, Dynamic Stagnation Points, disease onset time Motion-Qi, and corresponding treatment principles based on birth time and current time. These calculations provide us with a "temporal dimension" diagnostic framework — from the perspective of time, predicting an individual's constitutional weaknesses, disease tendencies, onset times, and treatment principles.

However, **Five Motions and Six Qi calculation is only one dimension of diagnosis, not the whole**. To make an accurate diagnosis, it is also necessary to combine the traditional TCM "Four Diagnostic Methods" — inspection, auscultation-olfaction, inquiry, and palpation — to verify and supplement the Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results from the "spatial dimension" (actual manifestations of the body).

This is like weather forecasting: Five Motions and Six Qi calculation is "long-term weather forecasting" (predicting climate trends based on astronomical laws), and the Four Diagnostic Methods are "on-site meteorological observation" (actually measuring current weather conditions with thermometers, hygrometers, anemometers, etc.). Only by combining "long-term forecasting" and "on-site observation" can the most accurate judgment be made.

### 9.2 Relationship Between Five Motions and Six Qi Calculation and Four Diagnostic Methods

The relationship between Five Motions and Six Qi calculation and the Four Diagnostic Methods can be summarized in the following three points:

**First, calculation is the "substance," Four Diagnostic Methods are the "function."**
- Five Motions and Six Qi calculation provides a "theoretical framework" — starting from temporal laws, predicting an individual's possible constitutional weaknesses and disease tendencies. This is the "substance" (fundamental, theory).
- The Four Diagnostic Methods provide "actual evidence" — by observing the actual manifestations of the body, verifying whether the calculation results are correct, and discovering problems not covered by the calculation. This is the "function" (application, practice).
- The combination of both is "substance-function unity" — theory guides practice, practice verifies theory.

**Second, calculation is the "constant," Four Diagnostic Methods are the "change."**
- Five Motions and Six Qi calculation describes the "constant state" — most people born at a certain time will have a certain constitutional tendency. This is the "constant" (universal law).
- What the Four Diagnostic Methods discover is the "changed state" — due to differences in genetics, environment, lifestyle, and other factors, each person's actual manifestations may differ from the calculated "constant state." This is the "change" (individual differences).
- The combination of both is "constant-change combination" — on the basis of universal laws, fully considering individual differences.

**Third, calculation is "prediction," Four Diagnostic Methods are "verification."**
- Five Motions and Six Qi calculation is "prediction" — before disease onset, predicting possible diseases and onset times.
- The Four Diagnostic Methods are "verification" — at or after disease onset, verifying whether the prediction is correct through body manifestations.
- The combination of both is a "prediction-verification-correction" cycle — continuously using Four Diagnostic Methods results to verify calculation results, and correcting the calculation model based on verification results, gradually improving prediction accuracy.

### 9.3 Core Principles of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis

The core principle of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis is: **"guided by calculation, based on Four Diagnostic Methods, synthesize and diagnose."**

Specifically:

1. **Guided by calculation**: First perform Five Motions and Six Qi calculation to obtain preliminary predictions such as constitutional weaknesses, disease tendencies, and onset times, serving as the "guide" for the Four Diagnostic Methods — focus on examining parts and symptoms related to the calculation results.
2. **Based on Four Diagnostic Methods**: Then perform the Four Diagnostic Methods to collect the actual manifestations of the body, serving as the "basis" for diagnosis — Four Diagnostic Methods results are the decisive evidence for the final diagnosis.
3. **Synthesize and diagnose**: Finally, comprehensively judge the calculation results and Four Diagnostic Methods results — if the two are consistent, the diagnosis has high credibility; if inconsistent, need to carefully analyze the reasons (is the calculation inaccurate, or are the Four Diagnostic Methods incomplete, or is it due to individual differences), and if necessary, perform further examinations.

## 2. Combination of Inspection and Five Motions and Six Qi

### 9.4 Core Contents of Inspection

Inspection is a diagnostic method in which a doctor visually observes the patient's spirit, complexion, body shape, tongue manifestation, facial color, etc., to understand the condition. The core contents of inspection include:

- **Inspecting spirit**: Observing the patient's mental state, consciousness, eye expression, etc., to judge the exuberance or deficiency of righteous qi.
- **Inspecting color**: Observing the patient's facial color, skin color, lip color, etc., to judge the exuberance or deficiency of qi-blood and the nature of evil qi.
- **Inspecting form**: Observing the patient's body type, posture, movements, etc., to judge the strength of constitution and the severity of the condition.
- **Inspecting tongue**: Observing the patient's tongue substance, tongue coating, tongue shape, tongue state, etc., to judge the deficiency or excess of zang-fu organs and the nature of evil qi.
- **Inspecting excretions**: Observing the patient's phlegm, nasal mucus, saliva, spittle, vomitus, urine and stool, etc., to judge the nature of evil qi and the severity of the condition.

## 3. Combination of Auscultation-Olfaction and Five Motions and Six Qi

### 9.8 Core Contents of Auscultation-Olfaction

Auscultation-olfaction is a diagnostic method in which a doctor uses hearing and smell to observe the patient's voice, breathing, cough, vomiting, hiccup, belching, and other sounds, as well as breath odor, body odor, secretion odor, etc., to understand the condition. The core contents of auscultation-olfaction include:

- **Listening to sounds**: Observing the pitch, strength, clarity, urgency, etc., of the patient's voice, breathing, cough, vomiting, hiccup, belching, and other sounds.
- **Smelling odors**: Observing the patient's breath odor, body odor, secretion (phlegm, nasal mucus, saliva, spittle, urine and stool, etc.) odor.

## 4. Combination of Inquiry and Five Motions and Six Qi

### 9.11 Core Contents of Inquiry

Inquiry is a diagnostic method in which a doctor asks the patient or family members to understand the patient's medical history, symptoms, lifestyle habits, family history, etc., to diagnose disease. The core contents of inquiry include:

- **Asking general information**: Name, gender, age, occupation, native place, address, etc.
- **Asking chief complaint**: The patient's main suffering or symptom, and duration.
- **Asking present illness history**: The occurrence, development, treatment process, current symptoms, etc., of the disease.
- **Asking past history**: Past health status, disease history, surgical history, trauma history, allergy history, etc.
- **Asking personal history**: Lifestyle habits, dietary preferences, sleep status, exercise status, tobacco and alcohol habits, emotional state, marital and childbirth history, etc.
- **Asking family history**: Health status, disease history, genetic disease history, etc., of family members.

### 9.12 Combination of Medical History and Five Motions and Six Qi

Medical history (especially past disease history and onset time) has a close relationship with Five Motions and Six Qi. By asking the patient's past disease history and onset time, the accuracy of Five Motions and Six Qi calculation can be verified:

**Verification Method**:
1. First, based on the patient's birth time, calculate their congenital endowment (Core Organs, Congenital Gap, High-Risk Period, Exacerbation Period, etc.).
2. Then ask the patient's past disease history — what diseases have they had? What were the onset times of these diseases?
3. Compare the patient's past diseases and onset times with the calculated Core Organs, High-Risk Period, and Exacerbation Period:
   - If the diseases the patient had in the past are indeed related to the calculated Core Organs (e.g., calculated Core Organs = Heart, patient had myocarditis, arrhythmia, etc. in the past), it verifies the calculation result.
   - If the patient's disease onset time is indeed within the calculated High-Risk Period or Exacerbation Period, it further verifies the calculation result.
   - If inconsistent, need to analyze the reasons (is the calculation inaccurate, or is the patient's individual difference large, or are there other factors affecting).

**Example**:
- Patient born on June 15, 1990 (Geng Wu year, Ren Wu month, Xin Hai day, Gui Si hour).
- Calculation results: Core Organs = Heart/Small Intestine (principal, Six Qi Five Elements = Fire), Affected Meridians three-layer logic: First layer = Heart Meridian/Heart (direct correspondence), Second layer = Heart Meridian + Kidney Meridian/Heart + Kidney (triggered by Six Qi attribute), Third layer = Heart-Kidney non-interaction (transmission); High-Risk Period = Grain Buds-Major Heat (mid-May-late July), Exacerbation Period = around Start of Winter-Major Snow (November-December).
- Asking medical history: Patient had "viral myocarditis" in June 2015 (after Grain Buds), and was hospitalized for "arrhythmia" in November 2020 (after Start of Winter).
- Comparative verification:
  - Diseases (myocarditis, arrhythmia) consistent with Core Organs (Heart) ✓
  - Onset in June 2015, within High-Risk Period (Grain Buds-Major Heat) ✓
  - Onset in November 2020, within Exacerbation Period (Start of Winter-Major Snow) ✓
  - Patient simultaneously has symptoms such as insomnia, dreaminess, soreness and weakness of waist and knees, consistent with the third layer of Affected Meridians (Heart-Kidney non-interaction) ✓
- Conclusion: Calculation results highly consistent with medical history, verifying the accuracy of Five Motions and Six Qi calculation.

[Note: The above example is only for demonstration, actual verification requires large-sample clinical data.]

## 5. Combination of Palpation and Five Motions and Six Qi

### 9.15 Core Contents of Palpation

Palpation is a diagnostic method in which a doctor touches or presses the patient's pulse, skin, hands and feet, chest and abdomen, acupoints, etc., with the hand to understand the condition. The core contents of palpation include:

- **Pulse diagnosis**: Touching the patient's pulse (usually the radial artery), judging the floating or sinking, slow or rapid, deficiency or excess, slippery or choppy, etc., of the pulse condition.
- **Abdominal diagnosis**: Pressing the patient's abdomen, judging the softness or hardness, tenderness, masses, distension, etc., of the abdomen.
- **Pressing diagnosis**: Pressing the patient's skin, hands and feet, acupoints, etc., judging temperature, humidity, elasticity, tenderness, etc.

## 6. Comprehensive Judgment of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis

### 9.18 Method of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis

The core of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis is to comprehensively analyze the results of the four diagnostic methods of inspection, auscultation-olfaction, inquiry, and palpation, together with the calculation results of Five Motions and Six Qi, to obtain the final diagnosis.

**Steps of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis**:

1. **Step 1: Five Motions and Six Qi Calculation**
   - Based on the patient's birth time and current time, calculate congenital endowment, Dynamic Stagnation Points, disease onset time Motion-Qi, etc.
   - Obtain preliminary diagnostic hypotheses: What are the Core Organs? Where is the Congenital Gap? What is the disease tendency? When are the High-Risk Period and Exacerbation Period?

2. **Step 2: Targeted Four Diagnostic Methods**
   - Based on the calculation results, perform the Four Diagnostic Methods in a targeted manner:
     - Inspection: Focus on observing facial color and tongue manifestation related to Core Organs.
     - Auscultation-olfaction: Focus on listening to sounds and coughs related to Core Organs.
     - Inquiry: Focus on asking symptoms, medical history, and lifestyle habits related to Core Organs.
     - Palpation: Focus on feeling pulse conditions and abdominal signs related to Core Organs.

3. **Step 3: Comparison of Four Diagnostic Methods Results and Calculation Results**
   - Compare the actual manifestations collected by the Four Diagnostic Methods with the calculation results:
     - Consistent: Four Diagnostic Methods results support the calculation results.
     - Inconsistent: Four Diagnostic Methods results contradict the calculation results.
     - Partially consistent: Four Diagnostic Methods results partially support the calculation results, partially do not.

4. **Step 4: Comprehensive Judgment**
   - Based on the comparison results, perform comprehensive judgment:
     - If consistent: Diagnosis has high credibility, can be treated according to the calculation results.
     - If inconsistent: Need to carefully analyze the reasons, if necessary, perform further examinations, cannot blindly treat according to the calculation results.
     - If partially consistent: Use Four Diagnostic Methods results as the main basis, calculation results as reference, comprehensive judgment.

### 9.19 Handling When Calculation Results and Four Diagnostic Methods Results Are Inconsistent

When calculation results and Four Diagnostic Methods results are inconsistent, do not easily deny either party, but should carefully analyze the reasons. Common reasons and handling methods include:

**Reason 1: Inaccurate Calculation**
- It may be that the calculation algorithm has defects, or the parameter settings are unreasonable, leading to inaccurate calculation results.
- Handling method: Record this inconsistent situation as a basis for correcting the calculation algorithm. Through large-sample clinical data, continuously optimize the calculation algorithm.

**Reason 2: Incomplete Four Diagnostic Methods**
- It may be that the doctor's Four Diagnostic Methods are not comprehensive enough, missing some important signs or symptoms, leading to incomplete Four Diagnostic Methods results.
- Handling method: Re-perform comprehensive Four Diagnostic Methods, focusing on examining parts and symptoms that may have been missed.

**Reason 3: Individual Differences**
- Five Motions and Six Qi calculation describes the "constant state" (tendency of most people), but due to differences in genetics, environment, lifestyle, and other factors, each person's actual manifestations may differ from the "constant state."
- Handling method: Fully consider individual differences, use Four Diagnostic Methods results as the main basis, calculation results as reference.

**Reason 4: Disease Transmission**
- Disease is dynamically developing and may transmit from one zang-fu organ to another (e.g., "Wood generates Fire," Liver stagnation transforming into fire leading to Heart fire excessive), causing the current manifestations to be inconsistent with the initial calculation.
- Handling method: Carefully analyze the disease transmission law, judge which stage it is currently in, and then perform corresponding treatment.

**Reason 5: Influence of Acquired Factors**
- Acquired lifestyle habits, environment, emotions, treatment, and other factors may have largely changed the manifestations of congenital endowment.
- Handling method: Ask in detail about the patient's acquired factors, evaluate their influence on congenital endowment, and then comprehensively judge.

**Handling Principle**: When calculation results and Four Diagnostic Methods results are inconsistent, **use Four Diagnostic Methods results as the main diagnostic basis, calculation results as reference**. Because the Four Diagnostic Methods are direct observations of the patient's current actual state and are the most reliable diagnostic basis; while calculation is only prediction based on temporal laws and may have errors.

## 7. Mutual Verification of Five Motions and Six Qi Calculation and Four Diagnostic Methods Results

### 9.20 Significance of Mutual Verification

The mutual verification of Five Motions and Six Qi calculation and Four Diagnostic Methods results has important significance:

**First, improve diagnostic accuracy.**
- Five Motions and Six Qi calculation provides diagnostic reference from the "temporal dimension," and the Four Diagnostic Methods provide diagnostic basis from the "spatial dimension." The mutual verification of both can reduce the errors of a single diagnostic method and improve diagnostic accuracy.

**Second, discover potential problems.**
- Five Motions and Six Qi calculation can predict problems that the patient "may have but has not yet manifested" (such as Congenital Gap, potential constitutional weaknesses), and the Four Diagnostic Methods can discover problems that the patient "has already manifested." The combination of both can more comprehensively understand the patient's health status and discover potential problems.

**Third, predict disease development trends.**
- Five Motions and Six Qi calculation can predict disease onset time, exacerbation time, and remission time, and the Four Diagnostic Methods can observe the current state of the disease. The combination of both can more accurately predict disease development trends and take preventive measures in advance.

**Fourth, verify and optimize calculation algorithms.**
- By comparing calculation results with Four Diagnostic Methods results (actual manifestations), the accuracy of calculation algorithms can be verified, and calculation algorithms can be continuously optimized based on comparison results. This is a "prediction-verification-correction" cycle, gradually improving calculation accuracy.

### 9.21 Method of Mutual Verification

**Verification Method**:

1. **Collect large-sample clinical data**: Collect data such as birth time, Four Diagnostic Methods results, disease history, and treatment effects of a large number of patients.
2. **Perform Five Motions and Six Qi Calculation**: Based on each patient's birth time, perform Five Motions and Six Qi calculation to obtain predictions such as Core Organs, Congenital Gap, High-Risk Period, and Exacerbation Period.
3. **Compare calculation results with Four Diagnostic Methods results**:
   - Core Organs vs main diseased zang-fu organs discovered by Four Diagnostic Methods
   - Congenital Gap vs constitutional weaknesses discovered by Four Diagnostic Methods
   - High-Risk Period/Exacerbation Period vs actual disease onset/exacerbation time
4. **Statistically verify results**: Count the proportion (accuracy rate) of consistent calculation results and Four Diagnostic Methods results, as well as inconsistent situations and reasons.
5. **Optimize calculation algorithms**: Based on verification results, optimize calculation algorithms (adjust parameters, correct logic, add variables, etc.) to improve calculation accuracy.

**Verification Indicators**:
- **Accuracy rate**: The proportion of consistent calculation results and Four Diagnostic Methods results.
- **Sensitivity**: The proportion of patients with problems that calculation can correctly identify.
- **Specificity**: The proportion of patients without problems that calculation can correctly identify.
- **Positive predictive value**: The proportion of patients predicted to have problems by calculation who actually do have problems.
- **Negative predictive value**: The proportion of patients predicted to have no problems by calculation who actually do not have problems.

Through these indicators, the accuracy of Five Motions and Six Qi calculation can be objectively evaluated and continuously optimized.

## 8. Falsifiability of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis

### 9.22 Falsifiable Predictions

Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis (combination of Five Motions and Six Qi calculation and Four Diagnostic Methods) is falsifiable because it can make clear, testable predictions:

**Prediction 1: Consistency Between Calculation Results and Four Diagnostic Methods Results**
- Prediction content: Core Organs, Congenital Gap, etc., calculated based on birth time should have a significant correlation with the main diseased zang-fu organs and constitutional weaknesses discovered by the Four Diagnostic Methods.
- Test method: Large-sample clinical study, collect patients' birth time and Four Diagnostic Methods results, compare the consistency between calculation results and Four Diagnostic Methods results.
- Falsification condition: If the consistency between calculation results and Four Diagnostic Methods results is not significantly higher than random level, then Prediction 1 is falsified.

**Prediction 2: Diagnostic Accuracy Under Calculation-Guided Four Diagnostic Methods**
- Prediction content: Performing Four Diagnostic Methods guided by Five Motions and Six Qi calculation should have higher diagnostic accuracy than conventional Four Diagnostic Methods without calculation guidance.
- Test method: Randomized controlled trial, randomly divide patients into "calculation-guided Four Diagnostic Methods group" and "conventional Four Diagnostic Methods group," compare the diagnostic accuracy of the two groups (consistency with gold standard diagnosis).
- Falsification condition: If the diagnostic accuracy of the "calculation-guided Four Diagnostic Methods group" is not significantly higher than the "conventional Four Diagnostic Methods group," then Prediction 2 is falsified.

**Prediction 3: Medical History Verification**
- Prediction content: High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods calculated based on birth time should have a significant correlation with the patient's actual disease onset/exacerbation time.
- Test method: Retrospective cohort study, collect patients' birth time and disease history, compare the consistency between calculated High-Risk Periods/Exacerbation Periods and actual onset/exacerbation time.
- Falsification condition: If the consistency between calculated High-Risk Periods/Exacerbation Periods and actual onset/exacerbation time is not significantly higher than random level, then Prediction 3 is falsified.

### 9.23 Possible Counterevidence and Corrections

If the predictions of Four Diagnostic Methods Synthesis are falsified, possible causes and correction directions include:

1. **Inaccurate calculation algorithm**: The Five Motions and Six Qi calculation algorithm may have defects and needs optimization.
2. **Subjectivity of Four Diagnostic Methods**: The Four Diagnostic Methods (especially pulse diagnosis and inspection) have a certain degree of subjectivity, and different doctors' judgments may be inconsistent, affecting verification results. Need to formulate standardized Four Diagnostic Methods operation specifications to improve the objectivity and consistency of the Four Diagnostic Methods.
3. **Large individual differences**: Five Motions and Six Qi calculation describes the "constant state," but individual differences may be large, leading to inconsistent calculation results and actual manifestations. Need to add more individual variables (such as genetics, environment, lifestyle, etc.) to the calculation.
4. **Influence of acquired factors**: Acquired lifestyle habits, environment, treatment, and other factors may have largely changed the manifestations of congenital endowment. Need to consider the influence of acquired factors in the calculation.
5. **Insufficient sample size or research design defects**: May be due to insufficient sample size, research design defects, etc., leading to inaccurate research results. Need to conduct large-sample, multi-center, rigorously designed clinical studies.

Regardless of the result, this kind of falsifiable prediction and testing is precisely the core of the scientific method.
