# Chapter 8: Treatment Principles of Five Motions and Six Qi — Six Qi Brake and Stagnation Point Treatment Rules

## 1. Overall Approach of Five Motions and Six Qi Treatment Principles

### 8.1 Core Logic of Treatment Principles

In the previous chapters, we learned how to calculate congenital endowment (Chapter 5), Dynamic Stagnation Points (Chapter 6), and Principal-Guest Motion and Qi at disease onset time (Chapter 7). These calculations tell us "where the disease is," "when it onsets," and "how severe it is." However, knowing where the problem lies is not enough — we also need to know "what to do."

This chapter will elaborate on the treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi — how to give specific treatment principles and health preservation suggestions based on the calculation results.

The core logic of Five Motions and Six Qi treatment principles can be summarized in the following four sentences:

1. **Treat where the Stagnation Point is** — Core Organs are where the disease lesion is located, and the core of treatment is to regulate Core Organs.
2. **Restrain what the Trigger Point is** — the Trigger Point is the initiator of attack, and the key to treatment is to restrain the Trigger Point ("brake"), reducing attack intensity.
3. **Consider Tong Shi substitution and backup** — if the Stagnation Point is substituted by Tong Shi, treatment needs to consider both the original Stagnation Point and the substitution point.
4. **Prevent when it onsets** — High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods are time periods when disease is prone to onset/exacerbate, requiring advance prevention.

The core of this treatment principle is "treatment based on syndrome differentiation" — according to each person's specific calculation results (congenital endowment, Dynamic Stagnation Points, disease onset time Motion-Qi), give personalized treatment plans, rather than "one prescription for a thousand people."

### 8.2 Three Levels of Treatment Principles

The treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi can be divided into three levels:

**First Level: Etiological Treatment (Treating the Root)**
- Targeting the root cause of disease — Trigger Point (attacker) and Stagnation Point (attacked party)
- Goal: Eliminate the root cause of attack, restore Five Elements balance
- Methods: Restrain Trigger Point (brake), support Stagnation Point (supplement deficiency), harmonize Five Elements

**Second Level: Symptomatic Treatment (Treating the Branch)**
- Targeting the specific symptoms of disease — abnormal manifestations of Core Organs and Affected Meridians
- Goal: Relieve symptoms, reduce suffering
- Methods: Regulate Core Organs, unblock Affected Meridians, symptomatic medication

**Third Level: Preventive Care (Treating Disease Before It Occurs)**
- Targeting the onset time of disease — High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods
- Goal: Prevent disease onset or exacerbation, promote recovery
- Methods: Intervene in advance before High-Risk Periods/Exacerbation Periods, adjust lifestyle, enhance constitution

These three levels complement each other — etiological treatment solves the fundamental problem, symptomatic treatment relieves current suffering, and preventive care prevents future onset. A complete treatment plan should include all three levels simultaneously.

### 8.3 Relationship Between Five Motions and Six Qi Treatment Principles and Traditional TCM Treatment Principles

The treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi are consistent with traditional TCM treatment principles, both based on the basic principles of "treatment based on syndrome differentiation," "supporting the righteous and expelling the evil," and "regulating Yin and Yang." However, the treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi have their unique features:

First, **temporal dimension**. The treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi particularly emphasize "time" — not only treating "what disease," but also treating "disease at what time." The same disease, onset at different times, may have different treatment plans.

Second, **individual dimension**. The treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi particularly emphasize "individual" — according to each person's birth time (congenital endowment), give personalized treatment plans. The same disease, different people (different birth times), may have different treatment plans.

Third, **predictive dimension**. The treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi particularly emphasize "prediction" — not only treating diseases that have already onset, but also predicting diseases that may onset in the future, and preventing them in advance ("treating disease before it occurs").

These unique features make the treatment principles of Five Motions and Six Qi have unique value in "temporal medicine," "personalized medicine," and "preventive medicine."

## 2. Stagnation Point Treatment Rules

### 8.4 Core Stagnation Point Analysis and Core Principles of Stagnation Point Treatment

In Chapter 6, we introduced the **dual-module structure** of Dynamic Stagnation Point Calculation — Core Stagnation Point Analysis (macro overall judgment from Annual Motion/Heavenly Stem angle) + Six Qi Brake Dynamic Stagnation Point Calculation (micro specific positioning from Six Qi/Earthly Branch angle).

**Core Stagnation Point Analysis** answers "which element is stagnated overall, and how severe is the stagnation," including:
- Main Stagnation Point (e.g., "Wood Stagnation")
- Stagnation Point type (excess stagnation/deficiency stagnation)
- Stagnation qi intensity (0-10 quantitative score)
- Disease risk (stable/medium/relatively high/high)

**Six Qi Brake Dynamic Stagnation Point Calculation** answers "specifically which Earthly Branch is the lesion, which organ is damaged, and which meridians are affected," including:
- Power Trigger Point, Core Stagnation Point
- Core Damaged Organ, First Affected Meridian (three-layer logic)
- Tong Shi Backup and Warning, High-Risk Period/Exacerbation Period

Stagnation Point treatment needs to combine the results of these two modules — **first judge the overall stagnation situation from a macro perspective (Core Stagnation Point Analysis), then locate the specific lesion and transmission path from a micro perspective (Dynamic Stagnation Point Calculation)**, and only by combining both can a complete treatment plan be given.

The core principle of Stagnation Point treatment is: **"For stagnation, unblock it; for deficiency, supplement it; for excess, drain it."**

Specifically:

- **For stagnation, unblock it**: If the qi mechanism at the Stagnation Point is stagnated ("stagnation"), it needs to be "unblocked" — use methods such as soothing Liver and regulating qi, activating blood and resolving stasis, resolving phlegm and draining dampness to unblock the stagnated qi mechanism.
- **For deficiency, supplement it**: If the righteous qi at the Stagnation Point is insufficient ("deficiency," common in years of Annual Motion Deficiency, Core Stagnation Point Analysis shows "deficiency stagnation"), it needs to be "supplemented" — use methods such as supplementing qi, nourishing blood, nourishing yin, warming yang to supplement the insufficient righteous qi.
- **For excess, drain it**: If the evil qi at the Stagnation Point is excessive ("excess," common in years of Annual Motion Excess, Core Stagnation Point Analysis shows "excess stagnation"), it needs to be "drained" — use methods such as clearing heat, draining fire, purging, promoting urination to clear the excessive evil qi.

**Adjust treatment intensity according to the stagnation qi intensity of Core Stagnation Point Analysis**:
- Stagnation qi intensity 0-3 points (mild stagnation): Mainly daily care, can cooperate with dietary therapy and acupoint massage, no need for drug treatment.
- Stagnation qi intensity 4-6 points (moderate stagnation): Mainly Chinese medicine regulation, cooperate with acupuncture, dietary therapy, and lifestyle adjustment.
- Stagnation qi intensity 7-10 points (severe stagnation): Need active treatment, comprehensive treatment such as Chinese medicine, acupuncture, and massage, if necessary, integrated Chinese and Western medicine.

### 8.6 Precautions for Stagnation Point Treatment

When treating Stagnation Points, the following points need to be noted:

First, **distinguish deficiency and excess clearly**. The same Stagnation Point may be "deficiency stagnation" (stagnation caused by insufficient righteous qi) or "excess stagnation" (stagnation caused by excessive evil qi). Deficiency stagnation is mainly supplemented, with unblocking as auxiliary; excess stagnation is mainly drained, with unblocking as auxiliary. If deficiency and excess are not distinguished and medication is blindly used, it may "make deficiency more deficient and excess more excessive," aggravating the condition.

Second, **consider generation and restriction**. The treatment of Stagnation Points should not only regulate the Stagnation Point itself, but also consider the Five Elements generation-restriction relationship. For example, the treatment of Wood Stagnation (Liver stagnation) should not only soothe the Liver, but also consider "when seeing Liver disease, know that Liver transmits to Spleen, so first strengthen Spleen" (Jin Gui Yao Lue), and consider regulating Spleen-Stomach.

Third, **pay attention to seasonal timing**. The treatment of Stagnation Points should also consider the current seasonal timing (season, solar term). For example, the treatment of Fire Stagnation (Heart stagnation) in Summer (Fire qi on duty) can appropriately increase the power of clearing Heart and draining fire, while in Winter (Water qi on duty), attention should be paid to protecting Heart yang, avoiding excessive cold-cooling.

## 3. Trigger Point Treatment Rules

### 8.7 Core Principles of Trigger Point Treatment

The Trigger Point is the "attacker" of disease — the root cause of initiating attack and leading to stagnation. The core principle of Trigger Point treatment is: **"For excess, drain it; for strength, restrain it; for exuberance, calm it."**

Specifically:

- **For excess, drain it**: If the evil qi at the Trigger Point is excessive ("excess," common in years of Annual Motion Excess), it needs to be "drained" — use methods such as clearing heat, draining fire, and purging to clear the excessive evil qi.
- **For strength, restrain it**: If the force of the Trigger Point is too strong ("strong"), it needs to be "restrained" — use "what I restrict" to restrain the Trigger Point (for example, if Wood is too strong, use Metal to restrain Wood; if Fire is too strong, use Water to restrain Fire).
- **For exuberance, calm it**: If the qi mechanism at the Trigger Point is too exuberant ("exuberant"), it needs to be "calmed" — use methods such as regulating qi, descending qi, and heavy sedation to calm the exuberant qi mechanism.

The essence of Trigger Point treatment is "braking" — applying the brakes to the attacker, reducing attack intensity, thereby reducing damage to the Stagnation Point.

### 8.9 Coordination of Trigger Point Treatment and Stagnation Point Treatment

Trigger Point treatment (brake) and Stagnation Point treatment (supplement deficiency/drain excess) need to be used in coordination, neither can be neglected:

- **Mainly Trigger Point treatment**: If the disease is in the "onset period" (strong attack, obvious symptoms), then mainly Trigger Point treatment — focus on braking, reducing attack intensity, relieving symptoms.
- **Mainly Stagnation Point treatment**: If the disease is in the "remission period" (attack weakened, symptoms stable), then mainly Stagnation Point treatment — focus on supplementing deficiency, enhancing constitution, preventing recurrence.
- **Equal emphasis on both**: If the disease is in the "protracted period" (attack and weakness coexist), then equal emphasis on both — braking while supplementing deficiency, attacking and supplementing simultaneously.

This treatment strategy of "treating both root and branch, attacking and supplementing simultaneously" embodies the essence of TCM "treatment based on syndrome differentiation."

## 4. Treatment of Tong Shi Substitution and Backup Warning

### 8.10 Treatment Principles of Tong Shi Substitution and Backup Warning

In Chapter 6, we introduced the concepts of "Tong Shi substitution" and "Tong Shi backup and warning":

- **Tong Shi substitution**: When the Stagnation Point Earthly Branch is not in the Four Pillars, another Earthly Branch belonging to the same Six Qi substitutes to assume the responsibility of the Stagnation Point.
- **Tong Shi backup and warning**: Even if the Stagnation Point is in the Four Pillars, the Tong Shi Earthly Branch serves as a "backup brake," jointly assuming regulatory function with the Stagnation Point; need to be alert to "concurrent wear" — when the Stagnation Point and Tong Shi are attacked simultaneously, regulatory ability drops significantly (correction coefficient = 0.5).

The treatment principle of Tong Shi substitution and backup warning is: **"Main and substitution considered together, backup not forgotten, wear warned."**

Specifically:

- **Mainly substitution point (when Tong Shi substitutes)**: During treatment, mainly focus on the actual Stagnation Point (Tong Shi substitution point) — because this is the location currently actually assuming the responsibility of stagnation.
- **Consider original Stagnation Point (when Tong Shi substitutes)**: At the same time, also consider the original Stagnation Point — because the original Stagnation Point is where the "root of disease" lies, although currently not in the Four Pillars, it may "reappear" at specific times (months/years corresponding to the original Stagnation Point), leading to disease onset or exacerbation.
- **Pay attention to intensity difference (when Tong Shi substitutes)**: The intensity of Tong Shi substitution is usually slightly lower than the original Stagnation Point (correction coefficient = 0.8), so the medication intensity during treatment can be appropriately reduced, but cannot be completely ignored.
- **Consider Tong Shi backup (when Tong Shi backup)**: Even if the Stagnation Point is in the Four Pillars, during treatment also consider the Tong Shi backup Earthly Branch — because Tong Shi is a "backup brake," jointly assuming regulatory function with the Stagnation Point, and regulating Tong Shi can enhance the system's regulatory ability.
- **Be alert to concurrent wear (when concurrent wear risk is high)**: If Tong Shi is also in the Four Pillars (concurrent wear risk = high), special attention is needed — when the Stagnation Point and Tong Shi are attacked simultaneously, regulatory ability drops significantly (correction coefficient = 0.5), disease is prone to onset or exacerbation. During treatment, need to simultaneously strengthen the regulation of both the Stagnation Point and Tong Shi, enhancing the system's overall regulatory ability.
- **Prevent concurrent wear in advance**: Before the arrival of the months corresponding to the Stagnation Point and Tong Shi (same Five Elements Earthly Branch month range), strengthen regulation in advance to prevent the occurrence of concurrent wear.

### 8.11 Specific Treatment Examples of Tong Shi Substitution and Backup Warning

**Example 1 (Tong Shi substitution)**: Original Stagnation Point = You (Yangming Dry Metal, Zheng Hua, Lung/Large Intestine), but there is no You in the Four Pillars, Tong Shi substitution = Mao (Yangming Dry Metal, Dui Hua, Lung/Large Intestine).

  - Acupuncture points: Fei Shu (BL13), Dan Zhong (CV17), Chi Ze (LU5), He Gu (LI4)
- **Consider original Stagnation Point (You)**: Around You month (8th month, White Dew-Cold Dew), strengthen the regulation of Lung/Large Intestine, because the original Stagnation Point (You) may "reappear" during this time period.
  - Start taking Lung-moistening and cough-stopping Chinese medicine or dietary therapy one week in advance
  - Avoid catching cold and dry environments, prevent respiratory infections
- **Pay attention to intensity difference**: Since Mao is Dui Hua (coefficient 0.7) and is a substitution (correction coefficient 0.8), the actual stagnation intensity is about 0.56 times the original Stagnation Point (0.7 × 0.8), so the medication intensity can be appropriately reduced, but cannot completely stop medication.

**Example 2 (Tong Shi backup and concurrent wear warning)**: Stagnation Point = Si (Jueyin Wind Wood, Dui Hua, Liver/Gallbladder), in the Four Pillars (Day Branch); Tong Shi = Hai (Jueyin Wind Wood, Zheng Hua, Liver/Gallbladder), also in the Four Pillars (Year Branch) → Concurrent wear risk = High.

  - Common Chinese herbs: Same as Si (Chai Hu, Yu Jin, Xiang Fu, etc.), but can appropriately increase the dosage

  - In months of Wood exuberance (Yin month + Mao month, 1st month + 2nd month) and time periods corresponding to Jueyin Wind Wood (Si month + Hai month), pay special attention to prevention
  - Start taking Liver-soothing and qi-regulating Chinese medicine 2 weeks in advance to enhance the system's regulatory ability
  - Avoid emotional fluctuations and overexertion, reduce attacks on Liver/Gallbladder
- **Enhance overall regulatory ability**: Since concurrent wear will lead to a 50% decrease in regulatory ability, during treatment need to simultaneously strengthen the system's overall regulatory ability —
  - Regulate Spleen-Stomach (Earth), because Earth is the mother of Wood (Wood restricts Earth, Earth deficiency makes Wood more prone to stagnation)

## 5. Treatment of Affected Meridians

### 8.12 Treatment Principles of Affected Meridians (Three-Layer Logic)

In Chapter 6, we introduced the calculation of "Affected Meridians" — adopting **three-layer logic**, from direct to indirect, from shallow to deep, progressing layer by layer:
- **First Layer (first, most direct)**: Meridians directly corresponding to Earthly Branches (twelve time period correspondence, e.g., Zi = Gallbladder Meridian)
- **Second Layer (triggered by Six Qi attribute)**: Two meridians corresponding to Six Qi (each Three Yin Three Yang corresponds to hand meridian + foot meridian, e.g., Shaoyin = Heart Meridian + Kidney Meridian)
- **Third Layer (transmission, deeper)**: Zang-fu organ transmission (e.g., Shaoyin Sovereign Fire → Heart and Kidney, Heart-Kidney non-interaction)

The treatment principle of Affected Meridians is: **"First treat the first layer, then regulate the second layer, finally manage the third layer, progressing layer by layer, blocking transmission."**

Specifically:

- **First Layer (directly corresponding meridian) first**: First treat the meridian directly corresponding to the Earthly Branch — this is the most direct and most superficial Affected Meridian of the disease, and is the entry point for treatment.
- **Second Layer (Six Qi corresponding meridians) as main**: Focus on regulating the two meridians corresponding to Six Qi (hand meridian + foot meridian) — this is the core Affected Meridian of the disease, and is the main direction of treatment.
- **Third Layer (transmitted organs) as root**: Finally regulate the deeper transmitted organs — this is where the root of the disease lies, and is the ultimate goal of treatment.
- **Block transmission**: By regulating each layer of meridians, enhance righteous qi, block the further transmission of disease from the first layer to the second and third layers.

### 8.13 Specific Treatment Example of Affected Meridians (Three-Layer Logic)

- Second Layer (triggered by Six Qi attribute): Hand Shaoyin Heart Meridian, Foot Shaoyin Kidney Meridian, Heart, Kidney (Shaoyin Sovereign Fire corresponds to Heart Meridian + Kidney Meridian)
- Third Layer (transmission): Heart and Kidney (Heart-Kidney non-interaction, Water-Fire non-coordination, deeper)

## 6. Treatment of Six Qi Brake

### 8.14 Treatment Principles of Six Qi Brake

In Chapters 4 and 6, we introduced the concept of "Six Qi brake" — the "braking" effect of Six Qi on Five Motion attacks. The Six Qi brake mechanism explains why the same congenital endowment has different disease onset situations in different years/seasons.

The treatment principle of Six Qi brake is: **"Enhance brake, reduce attack."**

Specifically:

- **Enhance brake**: By regulating the organs corresponding to Six Qi, enhance the braking effect of Six Qi on Five Motion attacks. For example, if the attacker is Wood (Liver), then can enhance the force of Metal (Lung) to brake Wood ("Metal restricts Wood").
- **Reduce attack**: By reducing inducing factors (such as emotion, diet, overexertion), reduce the intensity of Five Motion attacks.

### 8.15 Specific Treatment of Six Qi Brake

According to the Five Elements attribute of the attacker (Trigger Point), the specific treatment methods of Six Qi brake are as follows:

| Attacker (Trigger Point) | Brake Party (restricts attacker) | Methods to enhance brake |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------|
| Wood (Liver/Gallbladder) | Metal (Lung/Large Intestine) | Diffuse Lung and descend qi, enhance the force of Lung Metal, use Metal to restrain Wood |
| Fire (Heart/Small Intestine) | Water (Kidney/Bladder) | Nourish Kidney yin, warm Kidney yang, enhance the force of Kidney Water, use Water to restrain Fire |
| Earth (Spleen/Stomach) | Wood (Liver/Gallbladder) | Soothe Liver and regulate qi, enhance the force of Liver Wood, use Wood to restrain Earth |
| Metal (Lung/Large Intestine) | Fire (Heart/Small Intestine) | Clear Heart and drain fire, or warm and unblock Heart yang, enhance the force of Heart Fire, use Fire to restrain Metal |
| Water (Kidney/Bladder) | Earth (Spleen/Stomach) | Strengthen Spleen and dry dampness, enhance the force of Spleen Earth, use Earth to restrain Water |

**Note**: Enhancing the force of the brake party should pay attention to "moderation" — cannot excessively enhance, otherwise the brake party itself may become a new attacker, leading to new imbalance. For example, excessively enhancing Metal (Lung) to brake Wood (Liver) may lead to excessive Metal qi, in turn attacking Wood, causing new Liver stagnation.

Therefore, the treatment of Six Qi brake should be "moderately enhance, aiming at balance" — enhance the force of the brake party so that it can effectively brake the attacker, but not excessively, maintaining the dynamic balance of Five Elements.

## 7. Prevention of High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods

### 8.16 Prevention Principles of High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods

In Chapters 6 and 7, we introduced the concepts of "High-Risk Period" and "Exacerbation Period":

- **High-Risk Period**: Based on the Four Directions Five Elements of the Stagnation Point, the months corresponding to all Earthly Branches with the same Five Elements, disease is prone to onset.
- **Exacerbation Period**: Based on the Four Directions Five Elements of the Trigger Point, the months corresponding to all Earthly Branches with the same Five Elements, existing disease is prone to exacerbate.

High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods adopt **same Five Elements Earthly Branch month range**, rather than a single month:
- Wood exuberance = Yin month + Mao month (1st month + 2nd month, Spring)
- Fire exuberance = Si month + Wu month (4th month + 5th month, Summer)
- Earth exuberance = Chen month + Xu month + Chou month + Wei month (four-season months, about March-April, June-July, September-October, December-January)
- Metal exuberance = Shen month + You month (7th month + 8th month, Autumn)
- Water exuberance = Hai month + Zi month (10th month + 11th month, Winter)

Note: The Five Elements grouping of High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods is based on **Four Directions Five Elements** (direction/season Five Elements), because the Five Elements attribute of months/seasons is mainly determined by direction/four seasons, not Six Qi Five Elements.

The prevention principle of High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods is: **"Intervene in advance, prevent problems before they occur."**

Specifically:

- **Intervene in advance**: Before the arrival of High-Risk Periods/Exacerbation Periods (usually 2-4 weeks in advance), start taking preventive measures, rather than waiting until after disease onset to treat.
- **Prevent problems before they occur**: By regulating constitution, enhancing righteous qi, and avoiding inducing factors, prevent disease onset or exacerbation.
- **Cover the entire range**: Since High-Risk Periods/Exacerbation Periods are same Five Elements Earthly Branch month ranges (may include 2 or even 4 months), preventive measures need to cover the entire range, not just a single month.

### 8.17 Specific Preventive Measures for High-Risk Periods

**High-Risk Period** (Stagnation Point same Five Elements Earthly Branch month range):
- **Start 2-4 weeks in advance**: Take regulating Chinese medicine or dietary therapy targeting the Stagnation Point, enhance the righteous qi of the Stagnation Point, improve resistance.
- **Cover the entire High-Risk Period range**: Since the High-Risk Period may include 2 or even 4 months (such as Earth exuberance = four-season months), preventive measures need to continue throughout the entire High-Risk Period range.
- **Avoid inducing factors**: During the High-Risk Period, avoid factors that may induce disease, such as:
  - Wood Stagnation (Liver stagnation): Avoid anger, depression, staying up late
  - Fire Stagnation (Heart stagnation): Avoid excessive excitement, high-temperature environments, strenuous exercise
  - Earth Stagnation (Spleen stagnation): Avoid overeating, raw/cold and sweet foods, excessive thinking
  - Metal Stagnation (Lung stagnation): Avoid catching cold, dry environments, smoke irritation
  - Water Stagnation (Kidney stagnation): Avoid catching cold, overexertion, intemperate sexual activity
- **Strengthen monitoring**: During the High-Risk Period, strengthen monitoring of physical condition, such as blood pressure, heart rate, body temperature, symptom changes, etc., once abnormalities appear, seek medical attention in time.
- **Moderate exercise**: During the High-Risk Period, perform moderate exercise (such as walking, Tai Chi, Baduanjin), enhance constitution, but avoid overexertion.

### 8.18 Specific Preventive Measures for Exacerbation Periods

**Exacerbation Period** (Trigger Point same Five Elements Earthly Branch month range):
- **Start 2-4 weeks in advance**: Take "braking" Chinese medicine or dietary therapy targeting the Trigger Point, enhance braking force, reduce attack intensity.
- **Cover the entire Exacerbation Period range**: Since the Exacerbation Period may include 2 or even 4 months, preventive measures need to continue throughout the entire Exacerbation Period range.
- **Reduce stimulation**: During the Exacerbation Period, reduce factors that may stimulate the Trigger Point, such as:
  - Wood Trigger (Liver attack): Avoid anger, impatience, spicy foods
  - Fire Trigger (Heart attack): Avoid excessive excitement, high temperature, spicy stimulation
  - Earth Trigger (Spleen attack): Avoid overeating, sweet foods, excessive thinking
  - Metal Trigger (Lung attack): Avoid catching cold, dryness, smoke
  - Water Trigger (Kidney attack): Avoid catching cold, overexertion, cold-cool foods
- **Maintain treatment**: During the Exacerbation Period, maintain the original treatment plan, do not arbitrarily stop or reduce medication.
- **Seek medical attention in time**: If signs of disease exacerbation appear (such as symptom aggravation, abnormal indicators), seek medical attention in time and adjust the treatment plan.

### 8.19 Handling of Overlapping High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods

Sometimes, High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods may overlap (for example, the months corresponding to the Trigger Point and Stagnation Point are the same or adjacent), at this time the disease onset risk is the highest, requiring special attention:

- **Strengthen prevention intensity**: During the overlapping period, strengthen the intensity of preventive measures, such as increasing Chinese medicine dosage, increasing monitoring frequency, and more strictly avoiding inducing factors.
- **Seek medical attention in advance**: Before the arrival of the overlapping period, seek medical attention in advance, communicate with the doctor, adjust the treatment plan, and make preparations for response.
- **Avoid high-risk behaviors**: During the overlapping period, avoid any high-risk behaviors that may induce or exacerbate disease, such as overexertion, emotional agitation, overeating, staying up late, etc.
- **Prepare emergency medication**: If it is an acute disease such as cardiovascular disease, carry emergency medication (such as nitroglycerin) with you during the overlapping period, just in case.

## 8. Treatment of Five Motion Excess and Deficiency

### 8.20 Treatment of Five Motion Excess

The characteristic of **Annual Motion Excess** is: Annual Motion Five Elements is excessive, actively attacks "what I restrict," leading to "what I restrict" being suppressed and developing disease.

**Treatment principle**: **"Drain its excess, restrain what it overcomes."**

- **Drain its excess**: Clear and drain the excessive Annual Motion Five Elements (Trigger Point), reduce attack intensity.
- **Restrain what it overcomes**: Enhance the force of "what I restrict" (Stagnation Point), improve its resistance, reduce the damage from being attacked.
- **Consider the restricting party**: At the same time, also pay attention to regulating "what restricts me," because Annual Motion Excess may counter-insult "what restricts me" (for example, Wood Excess may counter-insult Metal).

**Example**: Wood Motion Excess (Liver qi excessive, attacks Spleen Earth):

### 8.21 Treatment of Five Motion Deficiency

The characteristic of **Annual Motion Deficiency** is: Annual Motion Five Elements is deficient, "what restricts me" actively attacks Annual Motion Five Elements, leading to Annual Motion Five Elements being suppressed and developing disease.

**Treatment principle**: **"Supplement its deficiency, support what it cannot overcome."**

Specifically:
- **Supplement its deficiency**: Supplement and tonify the deficient Annual Motion Five Elements (Stagnation Point), enhance its righteous qi, improve resistance.
- **Support what it cannot overcome**: Restrain "what restricts me" (Trigger Point), reduce attack intensity ("brake").
- **Consider what it generates**: At the same time, also pay attention to regulating "what I generate," because Annual Motion Deficiency may lead to "what I generate" also being deficient (for example, Wood Deficiency may lead to Fire also being deficient, because Wood generates Fire).

**Example**: Wood Motion Deficiency (Liver qi deficient, Lung Metal attacks Liver Wood):

- Support what it cannot overcome: Diffuse Lung and descend qi, restrain the attack of Lung Metal (Ma Huang, Xing Ren, Jie Geng, Su Zi)
- Consider what it generates: Clear Heart and nourish blood, prevent Wood not generating Fire leading to Heart fire deficiency (Dan Shen, Dang Gui, Suan Zao Ren)

## 9. Treatment of Six Qi Guest-Principal Superimposition

### 8.22 Treatment Principles of Guest-Principal Superimposition

In Chapter 4, we introduced the concept of "Guest-Principal Superimposition" — the superimposition relationship between Principal Qi (fixed) and Guest Qi (changing), including three types: same Qi, generation, and restriction, as well as the judgment of "compatible" and "incompatible."

The treatment principle of Guest-Principal Superimposition is: **"If compatible, harmonize; if incompatible, treat."**

Specifically:
- **Compatible (same Qi, generation)**: Climate is relatively normal, disease risk is low, mainly daily regulation.
- **Incompatible (restriction)**: Climate is abnormal, disease risk is high, need targeted regulation.
  - Guest Qi restricts Principal Qi (Guest overcomes Principal): Guest Qi is excessive, need to restrain Guest Qi ("brake").
  - Principal Qi restricts Guest Qi (Principal overcomes Guest): Principal Qi is excessive, need to drain the excess of Principal Qi.

### 8.23 Specific Treatment of Guest-Principal Superimposition

**Guest Qi restricts Principal Qi (Guest overcomes Principal)**:
- Characteristics: Guest Qi excessive, suppresses Principal Qi, climate abnormal (such as Summer should be hot but is cold, Winter should be cold but is hot)
- Treatment method: Restrain Guest Qi, support Principal Qi
- Example: Summer (Principal Qi = Shaoyang Ministerial Fire, Fire), Guest Qi = Taiyang Cold Water (Water), Water restricts Fire (Guest overcomes Principal), climate should be hot but is cold
  - Restrain Guest Qi (Water): Warm yang and dispel cold, use Fu Zi, Gan Jiang, Gui Zhi
  - Support Principal Qi (Fire): Warm and unblock Heart yang, use Gui Zhi, Gan Cao, Ren Shen

**Principal Qi restricts Guest Qi (Principal overcomes Guest)**:
- Characteristics: Principal Qi excessive, suppresses Guest Qi, climate abnormal (such as Spring should be warm but is hot, Autumn should be cool but is cold)
- Treatment method: Drain the excess of Principal Qi, consider Guest Qi
- Example: Spring (Principal Qi = Jueyin Wind Wood, Wood), Guest Qi = Taiyin Damp Earth (Earth), Wood restricts Earth (Principal overcomes Guest), climate should be warm but is windy
  - Drain the excess of Principal Qi (Wood): Soothe Liver and regulate qi, use Chai Hu, Yu Jin, Xiang Fu
  - Consider Guest Qi (Earth): Strengthen Spleen and harmonize Stomach, use Bai Zhu, Fu Ling, Chen Pi

**Same Qi (Guest-Principal same Qi)**:
- Characteristics: Guest-Principal same Qi, excess even more severe, climate extreme (such as Summer abnormally hot, Winter abnormally cold)
- Treatment method: Drain its excess, consider what it overcomes
- Example: Summer (Principal Qi = Shaoyang Ministerial Fire, Fire), Guest Qi = Shaoyin Sovereign Fire (Fire), same Qi, abnormally hot
  - Drain its excess (Fire): Clear Heart and drain fire, use Huang Lian, Zhi Zi, Lian Qiao

Fourth, **treatment of Tong Shi substitution and backup warning**. Proposed the principle of "main and substitution considered together, backup not forgotten, wear warned," elaborated on the specific treatment methods of Tong Shi substitution (mainly substitution point, consider original Stagnation Point, pay attention to intensity difference), as well as the treatment methods of Tong Shi backup and warning (consider Tong Shi backup, be alert to concurrent wear, prevent concurrent wear in advance, enhance overall regulatory ability).

Fifth, **treatment of Affected Meridians**. Proposed the principle of "main meridian as main, secondary meridian considered, block transmission," elaborated on the specific treatment methods of Affected Meridians.

Sixth, **treatment of Six Qi brake**. Proposed the principle of "enhance brake, reduce attack," elaborated on how to brake the attacker by enhancing the force of the brake party, as well as the precautions of "moderately enhance, aiming at balance."

Seventh, **prevention of High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods**. Adopting same Five Elements Earthly Branch month ranges (Wood exuberance = Yin-Mao, Fire exuberance = Si-Wu, Earth exuberance = Chen-Xu-Chou-Wei, Metal exuberance = Shen-You, Water exuberance = Hai-Zi), proposed the principle of "intervene in advance, prevent problems before they occur, cover the entire range," elaborated on the specific preventive measures for High-Risk Periods and Exacerbation Periods, as well as the handling methods when the two overlap.

Eighth, **treatment of Five Motion excess and deficiency**. Elaborated on the treatment principles and methods of Annual Motion Excess ("drain its excess, restrain what it overcomes") and Annual Motion Deficiency ("supplement its deficiency, support what it cannot overcome").

Ninth, **treatment of Six Qi Guest-Principal Superimposition**. Proposed the principle of "if compatible, harmonize; if incompatible, treat," elaborated on the specific treatment methods of three situations: Guest Qi restricts Principal Qi, Principal Qi restricts Guest Qi, and same Qi.

Tenth, **comprehensive treatment plan example**. Taking birth on June 15, 1990 and cardiovascular disease onset in May 2025 as an example, demonstrated the comprehensive application of Five Motions and Six Qi treatment principles (three levels of etiological treatment, symptomatic treatment, preventive care, as well as the arrangement of treatment cycle).

Eleventh, **falsifiability**. Clarified three falsifiable predictions (treatment effect, High-Risk Period prevention effect, Trigger Point treatment effect), as well as possible counterevidence and correction directions.
