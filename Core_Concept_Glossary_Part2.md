### 52. Congenital Gap (先天缺口) — [Original]

- **English functional description**: The weak five-element/organ in the birth year's five motions and six qi configuration, the constitutional weakness of a lifetime.
- **Detailed definition**: In the Five Motions and Six Qi configuration at birth, the Five Elements/zang-fu organ that is suppressed and weak is the constitutional weakness of a lifetime. Congenital Gap determines an individual's prone disease types and constitutional tendencies, and is the computable expression of "congenital endowment."
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Birth year Annual Motion + Sovereign Heaven + Earthly Deputy + Four Pillars
  - Operation: Find the most severely suppressed Five Elements/zang-fu organ
  - Output: Congenital Gap Five Elements + Zang-fu organ + Weakness degree
- **Source**: Original to this book, based on the endowment thought in "Suwen": "Humans are born from the qi of Heaven and Earth."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Congenital Gap has structural similarity to Developmental Origins of Health and Disease (DOHaD) theory and genetic susceptibility.

### 53. High-Risk Period (高发期) — [Original]

- **English functional description**: The month/solar term corresponding to the stagnation point branch, the time period when disease is most likely to onset.
- **Detailed definition**: The month/solar term corresponding to the Stagnation Point Earthly Branch, is the time period when disease is prone to onset. E.g., Stagnation Point = You (Yangming Dry Metal) → High-Risk Period is around Autumn Equinox (You month). High-Risk Period is the key intervention window for "treating disease before it arises."
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Stagnation Point Earthly Branch
  - Operation: Look up Earthly Branch-month correspondence table (Zi → 11th month, Chou → 12th month...)
  - Output: High-Risk Period month + Solar term range
- **Source**: Original to this book, based on the correspondence relationship between Earthly Branches and months.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — High-Risk Period is consistent with seasonal disease onset patterns; modern epidemiology has confirmed the seasonal onset of multiple diseases.

### 54. Exacerbation Period (加重期) — [Original]

- **English functional description**: The month/solar term corresponding to the trigger point branch, the time period when existing disease is most likely to worsen.
- **Detailed definition**: The month/solar term corresponding to the Trigger Point Earthly Branch, is the time period when existing disease is prone to worsening. E.g., Trigger Point = Zi (Shaoyin Sovereign Fire) → Exacerbation Period is around Winter Solstice (Zi month). Exacerbation Period is the key monitoring period for chronic disease management.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Trigger Point Earthly Branch
  - Operation: Look up Earthly Branch-month correspondence table
  - Output: Exacerbation Period month + Solar term range
- **Source**: Original to this book, based on the correspondence relationship between Earthly Branches and months.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Exacerbation Period is consistent with seasonal patterns of acute exacerbation of chronic diseases, such as COPD worsening in winter, high incidence of cardiovascular disease in winter, etc.

### 55. Attack Intensity (攻击强度) — [Original]

- **English functional description**: The intensity of the trigger point attacking the stagnation point, determined by annual motion excess/deficiency, five-element generation-restriction, and zheng-hua/dui-hua coefficients.
- **Detailed definition**: The attack intensity of the Trigger Point against the Stagnation Point, jointly determined by three factors: ① Annual Motion excess/deficiency (excess means strong attack, deficiency means strong being attacked); ② Five Elements generation-restriction relationship (direct restriction is strongest, indirect restriction is secondary); ③ Normalization/Opposition coefficient (Normalization = 1.0, Opposition = 0.7). Attack Intensity determines the speed of stagnant qi accumulation and the severity of disease.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Annual Motion excess/deficiency + Five Elements relationship + Normalization/Opposition
  - Operation: Attack Intensity = Base value × Five Elements coefficient × Normalization/Opposition coefficient
  - Output: Attack Intensity value (1~10)
- **Source**: Original to this book, based on the quantitative thought of Five Motions and Six Qi.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Attack Intensity has structural similarity to etiological exposure dose and pathogenic force intensity.

### 56. Brake Intensity (制动强度) — [Original]

- **English functional description**: The intensity of six qi restraining five motions, determined by the six qi type and its zheng-hua/dui-hua coefficient.
- **Detailed definition**: The restraining intensity of Six Qi against Five Motions, determined by Six Qi type and Normalization/Opposition coefficient. Brake Intensity and Attack Intensity jointly constitute the Motion-Brake Balance, and are important parameters for determining whether qi flow is unobstructed.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Six Qi type + Normalization/Opposition
  - Operation: Brake Intensity = Base value × Normalization/Opposition coefficient
  - Output: Brake Intensity value (1~10)
- **Source**: Original to this book, based on the dynamic balance thought of Five Motions and Six Qi.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Brake Intensity has structural similarity to negative feedback regulation intensity and inhibitory control signals.

### 57. Epidemic Warning (时疫预警) — [Original]

- **English functional description**: Early warning of epidemic diseases based on the year's five motions and six qi configuration, especially extreme configurations like Tai Yi Tian Fu.
- **Detailed definition**: Early warning of epidemic diseases based on the current year's Five Motions and Six Qi configuration (especially extreme years such as Supreme Heavenly Concordance, Heavenly Concordance, etc.). Epidemic Warning combines multiple factors such as Annual Motion, Sovereign Heaven, Earthly Deputy, Guest-Principal Superimposition, etc., to predict possible epidemic types and high-incidence periods of the year.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Complete configuration of the current year's Five Motions and Six Qi
  - Operation: Determine whether it is an extreme year + Analyze Six Qi exuberance type
  - Output: Epidemic Warning level + Possible disease type + High-incidence period
- **Source**: Original to this book, based on the discussion of the relationship between epidemics and motion-qi in "Suwen · Liuyuan Zhengji Dalun".
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Epidemic Warning has structural similarity to modern infectious disease monitoring and climate-related disease prediction.

## 6. Four Examinations and Eight Principles Symbols

Four Examinations and Eight Principles are the basic framework of TCM diagnosis. This book combines them with Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results for synthesis to improve diagnostic accuracy.

### 58. Four Examinations (Si Zhen)

- **English functional description**: The four diagnostic methods: inspection (望), auscultation-olfaction (闻), inquiry (问), and palpation (切), the foundation of TCM diagnosis.
- **Detailed definition**: Collective term for the four diagnostic methods: Inspection, Auscultation-Olfaction, Inquiry, Palpation. Four Examinations are the basic means of collecting clinical information in TCM. This book performs "Eight Principles synthesis" between Four Examinations results and Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results to improve diagnostic accuracy and reproducibility.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Patient's clinical manifestations
  - Operation: Collect information through Inspection, Auscultation-Olfaction, Inquiry, Palpation
  - Output: Four Examinations raw data → Eight Principles syndrome differentiation results
- **Source**: "Nanjing · Chapter 61": "Knowing by inspection is called divine; knowing by auscultation is called sage; knowing by inquiry is called skilled; knowing by pulse palpation is called clever."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Four Examinations have structural similarity to inspection-palpation-percussion-auscultation, history taking, and physical examination in modern medicine, and are the basis of clinical diagnosis.

### 59. Inspection (Wang Zhen)

- **English functional description**: Diagnosis by observing the patient's spirit, complexion, physical form, tongue, and secretions.
- **Detailed definition**: Diagnosis by observing the patient's spirit, complexion, physical form, posture, tongue manifestation, secretions, etc. Inspection has the highest weight among the Four Examinations, "knowing by inspection is called divine." In this book's program, Inspection includes the input of information such as tongue manifestation, facial complexion, mental state, etc.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Patient's visual manifestations
  - Operation: Observe spirit, complexion, physical form, posture, tongue manifestation
  - Output: Inspection data → Eight Principles syndrome differentiation input
- **Source**: "Suwen · Maiyao Jingwei Lun": "The essence and five colors are the splendor of qi."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Tongue manifestation and facial complexion in Inspection are correlated with physical sign observation and laboratory indicators in modern medicine; objectification research of tongue manifestation has made progress.

### 60. Auscultation and Olfaction (Wen Zhen)

- **English functional description**: Diagnosis by listening to the patient's voice, breathing, coughing, and smelling odors.
- **Detailed definition**: Diagnosis by listening to the patient's voice, breathing, coughing, hiccups, etc., and smelling the patient's breath, body odor, secretion odor, etc. Auscultation and Olfaction includes two parts: listening to sounds and smelling odors.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Patient's sounds and odors
  - Operation: Listen to sounds (voice, breathing, coughing) + Smell odors (breath, body odor, secretions)
  - Output: Auscultation-Olfaction data → Eight Principles syndrome differentiation input
- **Source**: "Suwen · Yin Yang Yingxiang Dalun": "Listening to voice sounds knows the suffering."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Sound analysis in Auscultation-Olfaction has structural similarity to modern speech analysis and breath sound analysis; odor analysis is related to electronic nose technology.

### 61. Inquiry (Wen Zhen)

- **English functional description**: Diagnosis by asking about the patient's chief complaint, history, symptoms, lifestyle, and environmental factors.
- **Detailed definition**: Diagnosis by asking about the patient's chief complaint, present illness history, past history, personal history, family history, lifestyle habits, environmental factors, etc. Inquiry is the most information-rich means among the Four Examinations. In this book's program, Inquiry includes multi-dimensional information such as symptoms, lifestyle habits, environment, etc.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Patient's verbal information
  - Operation: Ask about chief complaint, present illness history, past history, personal history, lifestyle habits
  - Output: Inquiry data → Eight Principles syndrome differentiation input
- **Source**: "Suwen · Shu Wuguo Lun": "Whenever diagnosing a disease, one must ask about diet and dwelling."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Inquiry is completely consistent with history taking in modern medicine, and is the core link of clinical diagnosis.

### 62. Palpation (Qie Zhen)

- **English functional description**: Diagnosis by touching and pressing the patient's pulse, abdomen, skin, and acupoints.
- **Detailed definition**: Diagnosis by touching and pressing the patient's pulse, abdomen, skin, acupoints, etc. Palpation includes two parts: pulse diagnosis and pressing diagnosis, among which pulse diagnosis is the most characteristic diagnostic method of TCM. In this book's program, Palpation is mainly based on pulse input.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Patient's pulse and body surface reactions
  - Operation: Pulse palpation (Cun-Guan-Chi, Fu-Zhong-Chen) + Pressing diagnosis (abdomen, skin, acupoints)
  - Output: Palpation data → Eight Principles syndrome differentiation input
- **Source**: "Suwen · Maiyao Jingwei Lun": "The subtlety lies in the pulse, which must be observed."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Pulse diagnosis has structural similarity to modern hemodynamics and cardiovascular function testing; objectification research of pulse diagnosis has made certain progress.

### 63. Eight Principles (Ba Gang)

- **English functional description**: The eight diagnostic principles: yin-yang, exterior-interior, cold-heat, deficiency-excess, the foundational framework of TCM syndrome differentiation.
- **Detailed definition**: Collective term for eight categories of syndrome differentiation principles: Yin-Yang, Exterior-Interior, Cold-Heat, Deficiency-Excess. Eight Principles are the basic framework of TCM syndrome differentiation; all syndrome differentiation methods (zang-fu organ syndrome differentiation, six-meridian syndrome differentiation, defense-qi-nutrient-blood syndrome differentiation, etc.) are based on Eight Principles. This book's program performs "synthesis" between Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results and Eight Principles syndrome differentiation results.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Four Examinations data
  - Operation: Classify by Eight Principles (Yin-Yang, Exterior-Interior, Cold-Heat, Deficiency-Excess)
  - Output: Eight Principles syndrome differentiation results → Synthesize with Five Motions and Six Qi results
- **Source**: "Shanghan Lun" laid the foundation for Eight Principles syndrome differentiation; Ming dynasty Zhang Jingyue's "Jingyue Quanshu" systematically elaborated.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Eight Principles have structural similarity to disease classification and condition assessment in modern medicine; e.g., Cold-Heat is correlated with inflammatory state, Deficiency-Excess is correlated with immune function.

### 64. Yin-Yang (Yin Yang)

- **English functional description**: The supreme principle of eight principles, classifying all diseases into yin syndrome and yang syndrome.
- **Detailed definition**: The overall principle of Eight Principles; all diseases can be divided into two major categories: Yin syndrome and Yang syndrome. Exterior syndrome, Heat syndrome, Excess syndrome belong to Yang; Interior syndrome, Cold syndrome, Deficiency syndrome belong to Yin. Yin-Yang is the commander of Eight Principles, "a good diagnostician, observing complexion and palpating pulse, first distinguishes Yin and Yang."
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Results of the other six principles of Eight Principles
  - Operation: Exterior/Heat/Excess = Yang, Interior/Cold/Deficiency = Yin
  - Output: Yin syndrome / Yang syndrome / Both Yin and Yang deficiency
- **Source**: "Suwen · Yin Yang Yingxiang Dalun": "A good diagnostician, observing complexion and palpating pulse, first distinguishes Yin and Yang."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Yin-Yang has structural similarity to sympathetic/parasympathetic nerve balance, oxidation/antioxidation balance, pro-inflammatory/anti-inflammatory balance.

### 65. Exterior-Interior (Biao Li)

- **English functional description**: The principle classifying disease location into exterior (表) and interior (里), indicating the depth and progression of disease.
- **Detailed definition**: The principle for distinguishing the depth of disease location. Exterior syndrome refers to pathogenic qi on the body surface (skin and hair, muscles and interstices, meridians), with relatively shallow condition; Interior syndrome refers to pathogenic qi deep into zang-fu organs (five zang and six fu), with relatively deep condition. Half-exterior half-interior syndrome is between the two. Exterior-Interior also reflects the transmission trend of disease (from exterior to interior is worsening, from interior to exterior is alleviating).
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Symptom location and characteristics
  - Operation: New disease, aversion to cold and fever, floating pulse = Exterior syndrome; chronic disease, only heat without cold or only cold without heat, deep pulse = Interior syndrome
  - Output: Exterior syndrome / Interior syndrome / Half-exterior half-interior syndrome
- **Source**: "Shanghan Lun": "Taiyang disease is characterized by floating pulse, stiffness of head and nape, and aversion to cold."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Exterior-Interior has structural similarity to infection site (upper respiratory tract/lower respiratory tract/systemic infection) and disease stage (acute phase/chronic phase).

### 66. Cold-Heat (Han Re)

- **English functional description**: The principle classifying disease nature into cold (寒) and heat (热), indicating the metabolic and inflammatory state.
- **Detailed definition**: The principle for distinguishing the nature of disease. Cold syndrome refers to insufficient Yang qi of the body or contraction of cold pathogen, manifested as aversion to cold, cold limbs, bland taste, loose stools, slow pulse, etc.; Heat syndrome refers to exuberant Yang qi of the body or contraction of heat pathogen, manifested as fever, thirst, flushed face, constipation, rapid pulse, etc. Cold-Heat also reflects the progression and regression of disease (cold transformation/heat transformation).
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Cold-Heat attributes of symptoms
  - Operation: Aversion to cold, cold limbs, bland taste, loose stools, slow pulse = Cold syndrome; fever, thirst, flushed face, constipation, rapid pulse = Heat syndrome
  - Output: Cold syndrome / Heat syndrome / Intermingled cold and heat / True cold false heat / True heat false cold
- **Source**: "Suwen · Yin Yang Yingxiang Dalun": "Yang excess causes heat, Yin excess causes cold."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Cold-Heat has clear correlation with inflammatory state (pro-inflammatory/anti-inflammatory), metabolic rate (high metabolism/low metabolism), autonomic nerve function (sympathetic/parasympathetic).

### 67. Deficiency-Excess (Xu Shi)

- **English functional description**: The principle classifying disease strength into deficiency (虚, weak righteous qi) and excess (实, strong pathogenic qi), indicating the balance between immunity and pathogen.
- **Detailed definition**: The principle for distinguishing the exuberance and decline of pathogenic and righteous qi. Deficiency syndrome refers to insufficient righteous qi (deficiency of qi, blood, yin, yang), manifested as fatigue, weakness, shortness of breath, spontaneous sweating, weak pulse, etc.; Excess syndrome refers to exuberant pathogenic qi (phlegm-fluid, blood stasis, food accumulation, qi stagnation, etc.), manifested as distension and fullness, pain, constipation, excessive pulse, etc. Deficiency-Excess also reflects the stage of disease (early disease mostly excess, chronic disease mostly deficiency).
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Pathogenic-righteous attributes of symptoms
  - Operation: Fatigue, weakness, shortness of breath, spontaneous sweating, weak pulse = Deficiency syndrome; distension and fullness, pain, constipation, excessive pulse = Excess syndrome
  - Output: Deficiency syndrome / Excess syndrome / Intermingled deficiency and excess / True excess false deficiency / True deficiency false excess
- **Source**: "Suwen · Tongping Xushi Lun": "When pathogenic qi is exuberant, it is excess; when essential qi is deprived, it is deficiency."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Deficiency-Excess has clear correlation with immune function (immunodeficiency/hyperimmunity), nutritional status (malnutrition/overnutrition), organ function (hypofunction/hyperfunction).

### 68. Syndrome Differentiation (Bian Zheng)

- **English functional description**: The process of analyzing four examination data to determine the syndrome (证), the core diagnostic method of TCM.
- **Detailed definition**: The process of determining the syndrome type (syndrome) of a disease by analyzing clinical data collected through Four Examinations. Syndrome Differentiation is the core of TCM diagnosis, including multiple methods such as Eight Principles syndrome differentiation, zang-fu organ syndrome differentiation, six-meridian syndrome differentiation, defense-qi-nutrient-blood syndrome differentiation, triple-jiao syndrome differentiation, etc. This book's program is based on Eight Principles syndrome differentiation, combined with Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results for synthesis.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Four Examinations data + Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results
  - Operation: Eight Principles syndrome differentiation → Zang-fu organ syndrome differentiation → Synthesize with Five Motions and Six Qi results
  - Output: Final syndrome type + Treatment suggestions
- **Source**: "Shanghan Lun" laid the foundation for syndrome differentiation and treatment.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Syndrome Differentiation has structural similarity to disease typing and precision medicine in modern medicine.

### 69. Treatment Method (Zhi Fa)

- **English functional description**: The therapeutic principle and method determined based on syndrome differentiation, the bridge between diagnosis and treatment.
- **Detailed definition**: Treatment principles and methods determined based on syndrome differentiation results. Treatment Method includes major therapeutic methods (such as the eight methods: sweating, vomiting, purging, harmonizing, warming, clearing, supplementing, resolving) and specific treatment methods (such as soothing Liver and regulating Qi, clearing Heat and draining Fire, activating Blood and resolving stasis, etc.). This book's program gives personalized Treatment Method suggestions based on Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results and Eight Principles syndrome differentiation results.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Syndrome type + Five Motions and Six Qi results
  - Operation: Determine Treatment Method based on syndrome type → Adjust combined with Five Motions and Six Qi
  - Output: Treatment Method + Formulas and herbs + Diet and daily life suggestions
- **Source**: "Suwen · Zhizhenyao Dalun": "Carefully abide by pathogenesis, each assigned to its category; seek it where present, seek it where absent; blame it where exuberant, blame it where deficient."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Treatment Method has structural similarity to treatment strategies in modern medicine (symptomatic treatment, etiological treatment, supportive treatment).

### 70. Prescription (Fang Ji)

- **English functional description**: The structured combination of herbs based on treatment principles, the main form of TCM treatment.
- **Detailed definition**: A prescription composed of multiple herbs according to the treatment principles and the formula composition principles of sovereign-minister-assistant-courier. Prescription is the main form of TCM treatment. This book's program has built-in 50+ classic prescriptions, recommending corresponding prescriptions based on syndrome differentiation results.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Syndrome type + Treatment Method
  - Operation: Look up prescription database, match corresponding prescription
  - Output: Recommended prescription + Composition + Usage + Addition and subtraction suggestions
- **Source**: "Shanghan Lun" and "Jinkui Yaolue" laid the foundation for prescription science.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Prescription has structural similarity to combination therapy and multi-target treatment in modern medicine; the multi-component multi-target mechanism of TCM compounds has been confirmed by modern pharmacology.

## 7. Zang-fu Organ and Meridian Symbols

Zang-fu organs and meridians are the basic framework of human body structure. Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results ultimately manifest in the lesions of zang-fu organs and meridians.

### 71. Five Yin Organs (Wu Zang)

- **English functional description**: Heart, Liver, Spleen, Lung, Kidney — the five solid organs that store essence (jing), the core of human physiology.
- **Detailed definition**: Collective term for the five solid organs: Heart, Liver, Spleen, Lung, Kidney. The common physiological function of the Five Yin Organs is "generating and storing essential qi," opposite to the Six Yang Organs (transmitting and transforming food). The Five Yin Organs are the core of human physiological activities, and Five Motions and Six Qi calculation results ultimately manifest in the lesions of the Five Yin Organs.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Five Elements attribute
  - Operation: Look up Five Elements-Five Yin Organs correspondence table (Wood → Liver, Fire → Heart, Earth → Spleen, Metal → Lung, Water → Kidney)
  - Output: Corresponding Five Yin Organ
- **Source**: "Suwen · Wuzang Bielun": "The so-called Five Yin Organs store essential qi without draining, hence they can be full but cannot be solid."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Five Yin Organs have structural similarity to organ systems in modern medicine (circulatory, digestive, respiratory, urinary, endocrine/immune), but TCM Five Yin Organs are functional units rather than anatomical organs.

### 72. Six Yang Organs (Liu Fu)

- **English functional description**: Gallbladder, Small Intestine, Stomach, Large Intestine, Bladder, Sanjiao — the six hollow organs that transmit and transform food.
- **Detailed definition**: Collective term for the six hollow organs: Gallbladder, Small Intestine, Stomach, Large Intestine, Bladder, Sanjiao. The common physiological function of the Six Yang Organs is "receiving and transmitting/transforming food," opposite to the Five Yin Organs (storing essential qi). The Six Yang Organs are exterior-interior paired with the Five Yin Organs (Liver-Gallbladder, Heart-Small Intestine, Spleen-Stomach, Lung-Large Intestine, Kidney-Bladder, Pericardium-Sanjiao).
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Five Yin Organ
  - Operation: Look up zang-fu organ exterior-interior correspondence table
  - Output: Corresponding Six Yang Organ
- **Source**: "Suwen · Wuzang Bielun": "The Six Yang Organs transmit and transform things without storing, hence they can be solid but cannot be full."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Six Yang Organs have structural similarity to the digestive system and urinary system in modern medicine.

### 73. Twelve Meridians (Shi Er Jing Mai)

- **English functional description**: The twelve main meridians corresponding to the twelve organs, the main pathways of qi and blood circulation.
- **Detailed definition**: Hand Taiyin Lung Meridian, Hand Yangming Large Intestine Meridian, Foot Yangming Stomach Meridian, Foot Taiyin Spleen Meridian, Hand Shaoyin Heart Meridian, Hand Taiyang Small Intestine Meridian, Foot Taiyang Bladder Meridian, Foot Shaoyin Kidney Meridian, Hand Jueyin Pericardium Meridian, Hand Shaoyang Sanjiao Meridian, Foot Shaoyang Gallbladder Meridian, Foot Jueyin Liver Meridian — twelve in total. The Twelve Meridians are the main pathways of qi and blood circulation, directly connected to zang-fu organs.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Zang-fu organ
  - Operation: Look up zang-fu organ-meridian correspondence table
  - Output: Corresponding meridian
- **Source**: "Lingshu · Jingmai" systematically elaborated the circulation of the Twelve Meridians.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Meridians have structural similarity to the fascial system and neuro-humoral regulation network in modern medicine; research on the essence of meridians is still ongoing.

### 74. Eight Extraordinary Meridians (Qi Jing Ba Mai)

- **English functional description**: Ren, Du, Chong, Dai, Yinwei, Yangwei, Yinqiao, Yangqiao — eight special meridians that regulate the twelve meridians.
- **Detailed definition**: Ren Meridian, Du Meridian, Chong Meridian, Dai Meridian, Yinwei Meridian, Yangwei Meridian, Yinqiao Meridian, Yangqiao Meridian — eight in total. The Eight Extraordinary Meridians do not directly belong to zang-fu organs, have no exterior-interior pairing, and their function is to regulate the qi and blood of the Twelve Meridians, "overflowing and storing" qi and blood.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Symptom location
  - Operation: Look up Eight Extraordinary Meridians circulation and indications
  - Output: Corresponding extraordinary meridian
- **Source**: "Nanjing · Chapter 27": "All these eight meridians are not restricted by the regular meridians, hence called the Eight Extraordinary Meridians."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Eight Extraordinary Meridians have structural similarity to neuroendocrine regulation networks.

### 75. Five Shu Points (Wu Shu Xue)

- **English functional description**: Jing (井), Ying (荥), Shu (输), Jing (经), He (合) — five special points on each of the twelve meridians, used for diagnosis and treatment.
- **Detailed definition**: Five specific acupoints below the elbow and knee joints on each of the Twelve Meridians: Jing-Well Point, Ying-Spring Point, Shu-Stream Point, Jing-River Point, He-Sea Point. The Five Shu Points are key nodes of meridian qi and blood flow, "where it emerges is Jing-Well, where it flows is Ying-Spring, where it pours is Shu-Stream, where it travels is Jing-River, where it enters is He-Sea."
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Meridian + Disease syndrome
  - Operation: Look up Five Shu Points indications (Jing-Well treats fullness below the heart, Ying-Spring treats body heat, Shu-Stream treats body weight and joint pain, Jing-River treats wheezing cough and cold-heat, He-Sea treats reversed qi and diarrhea)
  - Output: Corresponding Five Shu Point
- **Source**: "Lingshu · Jiuzhen Shiyuan": "Where it emerges is Jing-Well, where it flows is Ying-Spring, where it pours is Shu-Stream, where it travels is Jing-River, where it enters is He-Sea."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Five Shu Points have structural similarity to nerve segments and fascial connection points.

### 76. Yuan-Source Points (Yuan Xue)

- **English functional description**: The twelve source points where the primordial qi of each organ stays, used for diagnosis and treatment of organ disorders.
- **Detailed definition**: Specific acupoints near the wrist and ankle joints on each of the Twelve Meridians, are the sites where the primordial qi of zang-fu organs stays. Yuan-Source Points can reflect the functional state of zang-fu organs, used for diagnosis and treatment of zang-fu organ diseases. "When the Five Yin Organs have disease, one should select the Twelve Yuan-Source Points."
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Zang-fu organ
  - Operation: Look up zang-fu organ-Yuan-Source Point correspondence table
  - Output: Corresponding Yuan-Source Point
- **Source**: "Lingshu · Jiuzhen Shiyuan": "When the Five Yin Organs have disease, one should select the Twelve Yuan-Source Points."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Yuan-Source Points are correlated with autonomic nerve regulation points and low skin resistance points.

### 77. Luo-Connecting Points (Luo Xue)

- **English functional description**: The fifteen connecting points where the luo meridians branch off from the main meridians, used to treat disorders of both connected meridians.
- **Detailed definition**: Each of the fifteen Luo Meridians has an acupoint at the site where it branches off from the main meridian, called a Luo-Connecting Point. Each of the Twelve Meridians has one Luo-Connecting Point, plus Ren Meridian Luo-Connecting Point (Jiuwei), Du Meridian Luo-Connecting Point (Changqiang), Great Luo of Spleen (Dabao), totaling fifteen Luo-Connecting Points. Luo-Connecting Points can treat disease syndromes of both exterior-interior paired meridians.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Meridian
  - Operation: Look up meridian-Luo-Connecting Point correspondence table
  - Output: Corresponding Luo-Connecting Point
- **Source**: "Lingshu · Jingmai" elaborated the Fifteen Luo Meridians.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Luo-Connecting Points have structural similarity to neurovascular bundles and fascial intersection points.

### 78. Back-Shu Points (Shu Xue)

- **English functional description**: The twelve back points where qi of each organ infuses into the back, used for diagnosis and treatment of organ disorders.
- **Detailed definition**: Specific acupoints on the back where zang-fu organ qi infuses, located on the first lateral line of the Foot Taiyang Bladder Meridian. Back-Shu Points can reflect the functional state of zang-fu organs, used for diagnosis and treatment of zang-fu organ diseases, especially good at treating Five Yin Organ disease syndromes.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Zang-fu organ
  - Operation: Look up zang-fu organ-Back-Shu Point correspondence table
  - Output: Corresponding Back-Shu Point
- **Source**: "Suwen · Qifu Lun" elaborated Back-Shu Points.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Back-Shu Points have clear correlation with spinal nerve segment innervation, and are commonly used acupoints for acupuncture treatment of visceral diseases.

### 79. Front-Mu Points (Mu Xue)

- **English functional description**: The twelve front points where qi of each organ gathers on the chest and abdomen, used for diagnosis and treatment of organ disorders.
- **Detailed definition**: Specific acupoints on the chest and abdomen where zang-fu organ qi gathers. Front-Mu Points can reflect the functional state of zang-fu organs, used for diagnosis and treatment of zang-fu organ diseases, especially good at treating Six Yang Organ disease syndromes. Front-Mu Points used in combination with Back-Shu Points (Shu-Mu point pairing) can improve therapeutic efficacy.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Zang-fu organ
  - Operation: Look up zang-fu organ-Front-Mu Point correspondence table
  - Output: Corresponding Front-Mu Point
- **Source**: "Suwen · Qibing Lun" elaborated Front-Mu Points.
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Front-Mu Points are correlated with visceral referred pain areas and low skin resistance points.

## 8. Philosophical Symbols

Philosophical symbols are the worldview and methodological foundation of Five Motions and Six Qi theory. Understanding these symbols is necessary to understand the underlying logic of Five Motions and Six Qi.

### 80. Correspondence Between Heaven and Human (Tian Ren Xiang Ying)

- **English functional description**: The core worldview of TCM: human body is a microcosm of the universe, and human physiology/pathology corresponds to celestial and terrestrial changes.
- **Detailed definition**: The human body is a microcosm of Heaven and Earth ("the human body is a small Heaven and Earth"), and human physiological activities and pathological changes correspond to and influence each other with the changes of Heaven, Earth, and nature (seasons, climate, day and night, astronomy). Correspondence Between Heaven and Human is the worldview foundation of Five Motions and Six Qi theory; Five Motions and Six Qi calculation is the specific method of mapping Heaven-Earth changes to human health.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Heaven-Earth changes (Five Motions and Six Qi)
  - Operation: Map to human zang-fu organs and meridians according to Correspondence Between Heaven and Human
  - Output: Human physiological/pathological state prediction
- **Source**: "Suwen · Baoming Quanxing Lun": "Humans are born from the qi of Heaven and Earth, and formed by the laws of the four seasons."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Correspondence Between Heaven and Human has clear correspondence with chronobiology, seasonal epidemiology, and climate-health science; circadian rhythms and seasonal rhythms have been confirmed by modern science.

### 81. Divine Mechanism (Shen Ji)

- **English functional description**: The intrinsic life mechanism that governs growth, development, and transformation of all living things, rooted in the five elements and six qi.
- **Detailed definition**: The intrinsic, self-organizing, self-regulating life mechanism within living organisms, is the fundamental driving force of life activities. "Rooted within, called Divine Mechanism; when spirit departs, the mechanism ceases." Divine Mechanism is the intrinsic control center of life; Five Motions and Six Qi affect human health by influencing Divine Mechanism.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Five Motions and Six Qi configuration
  - Operation: Determine influence on Divine Mechanism (excess/deficiency → Divine Mechanism disorder)
  - Output: Divine Mechanism state (normal/disordered/exhausted)
- **Source**: "Suwen · Wuchang Zhengda Lun": "Rooted within, called Divine Mechanism; when spirit departs, the mechanism ceases."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Hypothetical correspondence — Divine Mechanism has structural similarity to self-organizing systems, epigenetic regulation, and neuro-endocrine-immune networks in modern biology, and is the intrinsic control mechanism of living systems.

### 82. Qi Establishment (Qi Li)

- **English functional description**: The extrinsic life mechanism that depends on external environmental qi (five elements and six qi) for survival, "rooted in the outside."
- **Detailed definition**: The mechanism by which living organisms depend on external environmental qi (Five Motions and Six Qi) to maintain life activities. "Rooted outside, called Qi Establishment; when qi ceases, transformation is exhausted." Qi Establishment is the interface for the exchange between life and environment; Five Motions and Six Qi affect the human body through Qi Establishment. Divine Mechanism (intrinsic) and Qi Establishment (extrinsic) jointly maintain life activities.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: External Five Motions and Six Qi
  - Operation: Determine whether Qi Establishment is normal (suitable environment → Qi Establishment normal, abnormal environment → Qi Establishment disordered)
  - Output: Qi Establishment state (normal/disordered/exhausted)
- **Source**: "Suwen · Wuchang Zhengda Lun": "Rooted outside, called Qi Establishment; when qi ceases, transformation is exhausted."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Qi Establishment has clear correspondence with environmental adaptation mechanisms, niche theory, and gene-environment interaction in modern biology.

### 83. Righteous Qi (Zheng Qi)

- **English functional description**: The body's defensive and self-regulating qi, including innate immunity, adaptive immunity, and homeostatic mechanisms.
- **Detailed definition**: The body's disease resistance and self-regulation ability, including Original Qi, Defensive Qi, Nutritive Qi, zang-fu organ qi, etc. Righteous Qi is the intrinsic foundation for maintaining health, "when Righteous Qi is stored within, pathogenic qi cannot interfere." "Deficiency" in Five Motions and Six Qi calculation corresponds to insufficient Righteous Qi, and "excess" corresponds to exuberant Righteous Qi.
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Five Motions and Six Qi configuration + Individual endowment
  - Operation: Determine Righteous Qi exuberance/decline (excess → Righteous Qi exuberant, deficiency → Righteous Qi insufficient)
  - Output: Righteous Qi state (sufficient/insufficient/exuberant)
- **Source**: "Suwen · Cifa Lun": "When Righteous Qi is stored within, pathogenic qi cannot interfere."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Righteous Qi has clear correspondence with the immune system (innate + adaptive immunity), homeostatic regulation mechanisms, and neuro-endocrine-immune networks in modern medicine.

### 84. Pathogenic Qi (Xie Qi)

- **English functional description**: Any factor that disrupts the body's normal function, including six climatic excesses (六淫), epidemic qi (疠气), emotional excess, improper diet, fatigue, and parasites.
- **Detailed definition**: Various factors causing disease occurrence, including external contraction of six excesses, epidemic qi, internal injury by seven emotions, improper diet, excessive or insufficient labor and rest, phlegm-fluid, blood stasis, stones, etc. Pathogenic Qi is the external condition for disease onset, "where pathogenic qi gathers, its qi must be deficient." Both "excess" and "deficiency" in Five Motions and Six Qi calculation can generate Pathogenic Qi (excessive qi, stagnant qi).
- **Operational rules**:
  - Input: Five Motions and Six Qi configuration
  - Operation: Determine Pathogenic Qi type and intensity (excess → excessive qi, deficiency → stagnant qi)
  - Output: Pathogenic Qi type + Intensity + Invasion pathway
- **Source**: "Suwen · Pingrebing Lun": "Where pathogenic qi gathers, its qi must be deficient."
- **Modern science correspondence**: Verified correspondence — Pathogenic Qi has clear correspondence with etiologies in modern medicine (pathogens, environmental factors, psychological factors, lifestyle).
