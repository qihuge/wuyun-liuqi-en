## 1. Why Docking with Modern Science is Needed

### 13.1 The "Language Barrier" of Five Motions and Six Qi

Five Motions and Six Qi theory was formed in ancient China over two thousand years ago, using ancient Chinese philosophical and medical language — Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, Five Elements generation-restriction, Sovereign Heaven-Earthly Deputy, victory-restoration-stagnation-eruption, etc. These concepts present a serious "language barrier" for modern people, especially those trained in modern science:

First, **different conceptual systems**. Modern science uses concepts such as molecules, cells, genes, proteins, signaling pathways, neural networks, etc., while Five Motions and Six Qi uses concepts such as Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, Five Elements Six Qi, Sovereign Heaven-Earthly Deputy, etc. The two conceptual systems are completely different and difficult to directly dialogue.

Second, **different methodologies**. Modern science emphasizes empiricism, quantification, reproducibility, and falsifiability, while traditional Five Motions and Six Qi research mostly stays at the level of theoretical interpretation and case reports, lacking systematic empirical research. The difference in methodology makes it difficult for Five Motions and Six Qi to be accepted by the modern scientific community.

Third, **different discourse systems**. Modern science has its own discourse system (academic papers, academic conferences, peer review, impact factors, etc.), while Five Motions and Six Qi research is mostly published in TCM journals, with less exchange with the mainstream discourse system of modern science.

This "language barrier" makes it difficult for Five Motions and Six Qi, despite its two thousand years of history and rich clinical experience, to integrate into the mainstream of modern science and gain broader recognition and application.

### 13.2 Significance of Docking with Modern Science

To break this "language barrier," it is necessary to dock Five Motions and Six Qi with modern science — use modern scientific concepts, methods, and discourse systems to re-understand, explain, and verify Five Motions and Six Qi. This has important significance:

- Through docking with modern science, the ancient concepts of Five Motions and Six Qi can be "translated" into language that modern people can understand, allowing more people (including those without TCM background) to understand the core ideas of Five Motions and Six Qi.

**Third, promote verification and application.**

- At the same time, the algorithms of Five Motions and Six Qi can also be applied to modern medicine and health management, developing new diagnostic tools, treatment methods, and health products.

## 2. Five Motions and Six Qi and Chronobiology

### 13.4 Overview of Chronobiology

Chronobiology is the science that studies temporal patterns in living organisms, mainly studying the generation mechanisms, regulatory factors, and physiological significance of biological rhythms.

**Classification of biological rhythms** (by cycle length):
- **Ultradian Rhythm**: Cycle shorter than 24 hours, such as heart rate, respiration, blood pressure fluctuations, hormone pulse secretion, etc.
- **Circadian Rhythm**: Cycle about 24 hours, such as sleep-wake cycle, body temperature fluctuations, hormone secretion rhythm, gene expression rhythm, etc.
- **Infradian Rhythm**: Cycle longer than 24 hours, such as menstrual cycle (about 28 days), seasonal rhythm (about 1 year), annual rhythm, etc.

**Molecular mechanism of biological clocks**:
- Modern biology has revealed the molecular mechanism of biological clocks — the core is a set of "clock genes" (Clock Genes), such as CLOCK, BMAL1, PER, CRY, etc., which generate about 24-hour rhythms through transcription-translation feedback loops (TTFL).
- These clock genes are expressed in various tissues and organs throughout the body, regulating the expression of thousands of genes, thereby affecting almost all physiological processes.

**Regulatory factors of biological clocks**:
- Light signals are the most important factor regulating biological clocks — light signals sensed by the retina are transmitted to the whole body through the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN, central biological clock), regulating peripheral biological clocks.
- Other regulatory factors include: temperature, feeding time, exercise, social interaction, etc.

## 3. Five Motions and Six Qi and Molecular Biology

### 13.7 Overview of Molecular Biology

Molecular biology is the science that studies life phenomena at the molecular level, mainly studying the structure, function, and interaction of biological macromolecules such as DNA, RNA, and proteins, as well as the regulatory mechanisms of gene expression.

**Core concepts**:
- **Gene**: DNA fragments carrying genetic information, the basic unit of heredity.
- **Gene Expression**: The process by which genes produce functional products through transcription (DNA→RNA) and translation (RNA→protein).
- **Epigenetics**: The study of heritable changes in gene expression without changing the DNA sequence, such as DNA methylation, histone modification, non-coding RNA, etc.
- **Proteomics**: The study of the composition, structure, function, and interaction of all proteins in cells, tissues, or organisms.
- **Metabolomics**: The study of the composition and changes of all metabolites in cells, tissues, or organisms.

**Technical means**:
- **DNA Sequencing**: Determining the base sequence of DNA.
- **DNA Microarray**: Simultaneously detecting the expression levels of thousands of genes.
- **RNA-Seq**: Determining the sequence and expression levels of the transcriptome (all RNA).
- **Mass Spectrometry**: Identifying and quantifying proteins.
- **CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing**: Precisely modifying DNA sequences.

## 4. Five Motions and Six Qi and Systems Biology

### 13.10 Overview of Systems Biology

Systems biology is the science that studies life phenomena at the system level. It no longer satisfies with studying individual genes or proteins, but studies the interaction networks between biological macromolecules such as genes, proteins, metabolites, and how these networks produce the overall behavior of organisms.

**Core ideas**:
- **Holism**: An organism is a whole, and the whole is greater than the sum of its parts. One cannot understand an organism merely by studying individual components; one must study the interactions and overall behavior between components.
- **Network Biology**: Biological systems can be represented by networks — nodes are genes, proteins, metabolites, etc., edges are their interactions. By studying the structure and dynamics of networks, one can understand the behavior of biological systems.
- **Nonlinear Dynamics**: Biological systems are complex nonlinear systems, and their behavior is often nonlinear — small changes may lead to large effects ("butterfly effect"), systems may have multiple stable states, may exhibit chaotic behavior, etc.
- **Emergence**: The overall behavior of biological systems (such as life, consciousness, disease) "emerges" from the interactions between components, and cannot be simply reduced to the properties of individual components.

**Research methods**:
- **Omics Technologies**: Genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, etc., simultaneously measure the states of thousands of molecules.
- **Bioinformatics**: Use computational methods to analyze and integrate large-scale biological data.
- **Mathematical Modeling**: Use mathematical equations to describe the dynamic behavior of biological systems, perform simulations and predictions.
- **Synthetic Biology**: Verify understanding of biological systems by artificially constructing biological systems.

### 13.13 Five Motions and Six Qi and Astronomy

Five Motions and Six Qi theory originated from ancient astronomical observations — Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, sixty Jiazi, twenty-four solar terms, etc., are all based on astronomical phenomena (movements of the sun, moon, planets). **Correspondence 1: Sixty Jiazi and celestial movement cycles.**
- Sixty Jiazi (60-year cycle) is the basic time unit of Five Motions and Six Qi.
- Modern astronomy has discovered that some celestial movements indeed have cycles of about 60 years or cycles related to 60 years:
  - **Jupiter-Saturn conjunction cycle**: Jupiter's orbital period is about 11.86 years, Saturn's orbital period is about 29.46 years, their conjunction cycle (Jupiter-Saturn conjunction) is about 19.86 years. Three conjunction cycles are about 59.6 years, close to 60 years.
  - **Saros cycle of solar and lunar eclipses**: About 18 years 11 days, three and a half Saros cycles are about 64 years, close to 60 years.
  - **Solar activity cycle**: About 11 years, 5-6 solar activity cycles are about 55-66 years, close to 60 years.
- The closeness of these astronomical cycles to the 60-year cycle of sixty Jiazi may not be a coincidence — ancient people may have discovered these cycles through long-term astronomical observations and integrated them into the sixty Jiazi system.

**Correspondence 2: Twenty-four solar terms and solar annual movement.**

- This indicates that the temporal division of Five Motions and Six Qi is based on precise astronomical observations and has a solid astronomical foundation.

**Correspondence 3: Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches and planets/stars.**
- In ancient Chinese astronomy, Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches have correspondences with planets and stars:
  - Heavenly Stems are related to the sun, moon, and five planets (seven luminaries) — Jia-Yi = Year Star (Jupiter), Bing-Ding = Mars (Mars), Wu-Ji = Saturn (Saturn), Geng-Xin = Venus (Venus), Ren-Gui = Mercury (Mercury).

### 13.19 Chronomedicine

Chronomedicine is a medical branch that studies the influence of temporal factors on health and disease, and is the application of chronobiology in medicine.

**Core contents**:
- **Circadian rhythm and disease**: Study the circadian rhythm of diseases (such as the morning peak phenomenon of myocardial infarction, nocturnal attacks of asthma), and adjust medication time according to circadian rhythm (chronotherapy).
- **Seasonal rhythm and disease**: Study the seasonal rhythm of diseases (such as high incidence of cardiovascular events in winter, high incidence of respiratory diseases in winter and spring), and seasonal prevention and intervention.
- **Age and disease**: Study the disease spectrum and physiological characteristics at different ages, and age-related disease prevention and treatment.
- **Chronotherapy**: Adjust medication time, dosage, and regimen according to biological rhythms to improve efficacy and reduce side effects. For example, antihypertensive drugs are more effective when taken in the early morning, statins are more effective when taken before bedtime, and chemotherapy drugs administered according to the temporal rhythm of the cell cycle can improve efficacy.

**Combination of Five Motions and Six Qi and chronomedicine**:
- Five Motions and Six Qi is essentially a broader and more refined chronomedicine — it not only considers circadian rhythm and seasonal rhythm, but also considers annual rhythm, 60-year cycle, and the interactions between these temporal rhythms (Principal-Guest Motions, Principal-Guest Qi, Guest-Principal Superimposition, etc.).
- Five Motions and Six Qi can provide richer temporal variables and more refined temporal predictions for chronomedicine, thereby developing more precise chronotherapy.
- For example, traditional chronotherapy mainly considers circadian rhythm (medication time in a day), while Five Motions and Six Qi can further consider seasonal rhythm and annual rhythm (which month, which year in a year needs to strengthen prevention and treatment), thereby achieving "full temporal dimension" personalized treatment.

- **Lifestyle medicine**: According to individual lifestyle (diet, exercise, sleep, stress, etc.) formulate personalized intervention plans.

### 13.21 Preventive Medicine

Preventive medicine is a medical branch that studies how to prevent diseases and promote health, emphasizing "treating disease before it arises" — taking measures before disease occurs to prevent the occurrence of disease.
