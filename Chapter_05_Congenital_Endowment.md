# Chapter 5: Congenital Endowment Calculation — Five Motions and Six Qi Configuration at Birth

## 1. Concept and Significance of Congenital Endowment

### 5.1 What is Congenital Endowment

The term "endowment" in Traditional Chinese Medicine refers to the constitutional characteristics and physiological traits an individual receives at birth. *Lingshu·Shou Yao Gang Rou* states: "When humans are born, some are hard and some are soft, some are weak and some are strong, some are short and some are tall, some are yin and some are yang." [To be verified: The original text needs to be checked] This indicates that the ancients long ago recognized that individuals have different constitutional characteristics at birth — some are hard, some are soft; some are weak, some are strong; some tend toward yin, some tend toward yang.

In the theoretical framework of Five Motions and Six Qi, **congenital endowment** refers to an individual's Five Motions and Six Qi configuration at birth, including: Annual Motion, Principal Motion, Guest Motion, Principal Qi, Guest Qi, Sovereign Heaven, Earthly Deputy of the birth year, as well as the Four Pillars Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches at the time of birth (year, month, day, hour). These configurations jointly determine an individual's "congenital constitution" — which zang-fu organs are relatively strong, which are relatively weak, which diseases are prone to occur, and which time periods are prone to disease onset.

### 5.2 Why Congenital Endowment is Important

The importance of congenital endowment is reflected in the following three aspects:

**First, congenital endowment determines an individual's constitutional weaknesses.** Different birth times correspond to different Five Motions and Six Qi configurations, and these configurations determine which of an individual's zang-fu organs are in a "suppressed" or "deficient" state at birth. These suppressed or deficient zang-fu organs are the individual's lifelong "constitutional weaknesses" — they are more prone to disease than other zang-fu organs and more susceptible to external factors.

**Second, congenital endowment determines an individual's disease susceptibility.** Due to the existence of constitutional weaknesses, individuals have higher susceptibility to certain diseases. For example, people born in a year of Wood Motion Excess have Earth (spleen-stomach) suppressed by Wood, so their spleen-stomach function is relatively weak and they are prone to digestive system diseases; people born in a year of Fire Motion Deficiency have Water (kidney) relatively excessive and Fire (heart) restricted by Water, so their cardiovascular function is relatively weak and they are prone to circulatory system diseases.

**Third, congenital endowment determines an individual's disease onset time patterns.** Five Motions and Six Qi not only determine "what diseases are prone to occur" but also "when they are prone to occur." Because Five Motions and Six Qi have a 60-year cycle, an individual's configuration at birth will "repeat" or "resonate" in subsequent years, and these time points are the "high-risk periods" when an individual's diseases are prone to onset or exacerbation.

### 5.3 Correspondence Between Congenital Endowment and Modern Science

The concept of congenital endowment has structural similarities with several concepts in modern science:

- **Developmental Origins of Health and Disease (DOHaD) theory**: This theory holds that environmental factors during the fetal period and early infancy (nutrition, hormones, stress, etc.) affect an individual's developmental programming, thereby affecting disease susceptibility in adulthood. This is consistent with the idea that "congenital endowment determines constitutional weaknesses."
- **Genetic susceptibility**: An individual's genetic makeup determines their susceptibility to certain diseases. This is consistent with the idea that "congenital endowment determines disease susceptibility."
- **Chronobiology**: Human physiological functions have multiple biological rhythms (circadian, seasonal, annual, etc.), and these rhythms affect the timing of disease onset and exacerbation. This is consistent with the idea that "congenital endowment determines disease onset time patterns."

Of course, the congenital endowment theory of Five Motions and Six Qi is not entirely equivalent to these modern scientific concepts — Five Motions and Six Qi emphasizes "temporal factors" (astronomical and climatic configuration at birth), while modern science emphasizes "genetic and environmental factors." But the core idea of both is consistent: **an individual's state at birth has a profound impact on their lifelong health.**

## 3. Calculation of Principal Motion and Guest Motion in Birth Year

Annual Motion only gives the "overall Five Elements tone" of the birth year, but the distribution of Five Elements within a year is uneven — different time periods have different Five Elements excess-deficiency. Principal Motion and Guest Motion are two parameters describing the distribution of Five Elements within a year.

### 5.7 Calculation Method of Principal Motion

**Definition**: Principal Motion is the fixed five-step motion each year, arranged in the order Wood→Fire→Earth→Metal→Water, with each step governing approximately 73 days. The "Tai-Shao" (Excess/Deficiency) of Principal Motion is determined by Annual Motion.

**Calculation formula**:
```
Input: Annual Motion Five Elements + Annual Motion Excess/Deficiency

Step 1: Determine the order of the five steps of Principal Motion: First Motion=Wood, Second Motion=Fire, Third Motion=Earth, Fourth Motion=Metal, Final Motion=Water
Step 2: Determine the Principal Motion step corresponding to Annual Motion (Wood=First Motion, Fire=Second Motion, Earth=Third Motion, Metal=Fourth Motion, Water=Final Motion)
Step 3: The Tai-Shao of this step = Annual Motion Excess/Deficiency (Excess=Tai, Deficiency=Shao)
Step 4: According to the rule of "Tai-Shao mutual generation," calculate the Tai-Shao of other steps
  - Tai generates Shao, Shao generates Tai (e.g., Tai Jue generates Shao Zhi, Shao Jue generates Tai Zhi)
Step 5: Five steps of Principal Motion = Five Elements + Tai-Shao

Output: Five Elements + Tai-Shao of the five steps of Principal Motion (First to Final)
```

**Tai-Shao mutual generation rule**:
- Tai (Excess) → generates → Shao (Deficiency)
- Shao (Deficiency) → generates → Tai (Excess)

For example:
- Tai Jue (Wood Excess) → generates Shao Zhi (Fire Deficiency) → generates Tai Gong (Earth Excess) → generates Shao Shang (Metal Deficiency) → generates Tai Yu (Water Excess)
- Shao Jue (Wood Deficiency) → generates Tai Zhi (Fire Excess) → generates Shao Gong (Earth Deficiency) → generates Tai Shang (Metal Excess) → generates Shao Yu (Water Deficiency)

### 5.8 Calculation Method of Guest Motion

**Definition**: Guest Motion is the changing five-step motion each year, with Annual Motion as the First Motion, arranged according to the order of Tai-Shao mutual generation. Guest Motion and Principal Motion are superimposed, jointly affecting the distribution of Five Elements in different time periods within a year.

**Calculation formula**:
```
Input: Annual Motion Five Elements + Annual Motion Excess/Deficiency

Step 1: Guest Motion First Motion = Annual Motion (Five Elements + Excess/Deficiency)
Step 2: According to the rule of "Tai-Shao mutual generation," calculate other steps
  - Tai generates Shao, Shao generates Tai
Step 3: Five steps of Guest Motion = Five Elements + Tai-Shao

Output: Five Elements + Tai-Shao of the five steps of Guest Motion (First to Final)
```

### 5.9 Calculation Example: 1990 (Geng Wu year, Metal Motion Excess)

**Principal Motion calculation**:
1. Principal Motion order: First Motion=Wood, Second Motion=Fire, Third Motion=Earth, Fourth Motion=Metal, Final Motion=Water
2. Annual Motion=Metal Excess, corresponding to Fourth Motion=Metal, therefore Fourth Motion=Tai Shang (Metal Excess)
3. Calculate according to Tai-Shao mutual generation:
   - Fourth Motion=Tai Shang (Metal Excess) → Final Motion=Shao Yu (Water Deficiency) (Tai generates Shao)
   - Fourth Motion=Tai Shang (Metal Excess) ← Third Motion=Shao Gong (Earth Deficiency) (Shao generates Tai)
   - Third Motion=Shao Gong (Earth Deficiency) ← Second Motion=Tai Zhi (Fire Excess) (Tai generates Shao)
   - Second Motion=Tai Zhi (Fire Excess) ← First Motion=Shao Jue (Wood Deficiency) (Shao generates Tai)
4. Five steps of Principal Motion:
   - First Motion: Shao Jue (Wood Deficiency)
   - Second Motion: Tai Zhi (Fire Excess)
   - Third Motion: Shao Gong (Earth Deficiency)
   - Fourth Motion: Tai Shang (Metal Excess)
   - Final Motion: Shao Yu (Water Deficiency)

**Guest Motion calculation**:
1. Guest Motion First Motion = Annual Motion = Tai Shang (Metal Excess)
2. Calculate according to Tai-Shao mutual generation:
   - First Motion=Tai Shang (Metal Excess) → Second Motion=Shao Yu (Water Deficiency)
   - Second Motion=Shao Yu (Water Deficiency) → Third Motion=Tai Jue (Wood Excess)
   - Third Motion=Tai Jue (Wood Excess) → Fourth Motion=Shao Zhi (Fire Deficiency)
   - Fourth Motion=Shao Zhi (Fire Deficiency) → Final Motion=Tai Gong (Earth Excess)
3. Five steps of Guest Motion:
   - First Motion: Tai Shang (Metal Excess)
   - Second Motion: Shao Yu (Water Deficiency)
   - Third Motion: Tai Jue (Wood Excess)
   - Fourth Motion: Shao Zhi (Fire Deficiency)
   - Final Motion: Tai Gong (Earth Excess)

**Principal-Guest Motion superimposition interpretation**:
- First Motion (Spring): Principal Motion Shao Jue (Wood Deficiency) + Guest Motion Tai Shang (Metal Excess) → Metal restricts Wood, Wood is suppressed by Metal, Liver qi insufficient in Spring, prone to depression, colds
- Second Motion (Summer): Principal Motion Tai Zhi (Fire Excess) + Guest Motion Shao Yu (Water Deficiency) → Fire excessive, Water insufficient, Heart fire excessive in Summer, prone to vexation, insomnia
- Third Motion (Long Summer): Principal Motion Shao Gong (Earth Deficiency) + Guest Motion Tai Jue (Wood Excess) → Wood restricts Earth, Earth is suppressed by Wood, Spleen-stomach weak in Long Summer, prone to indigestion
- Fourth Motion (Autumn): Principal Motion Tai Shang (Metal Excess) + Guest Motion Shao Zhi (Fire Deficiency) → Metal excessive, Fire insufficient, Lung Metal excessive in Autumn, prone to cough, constipation
- Final Motion (Winter): Principal Motion Shao Yu (Water Deficiency) + Guest Motion Tai Gong (Earth Excess) → Earth restricts Water, Water is suppressed by Earth, Kidney Water insufficient in Winter, prone to lumbar-knee soreness, tinnitus

## 4. Calculation of Principal Qi and Guest Qi in Birth Year

Principal Qi and Guest Qi are two parameters describing the distribution of Six Qi within a year. Similar to Principal Motion/Guest Motion, Principal Qi is fixed and Guest Qi is changing.

### 5.10 Calculation Method of Principal Qi

**Definition**: Principal Qi is the fixed six-step qi each year, arranged in the order Jueyin Wind Wood→Shaoyin Sovereign Fire→Shaoyang Ministerial Fire→Taiyin Damp Earth→Yangming Dry Metal→Taiyang Cold Water, with each step governing approximately 60 days.

**Calculation formula**:
```
Input: Current date (or the qi-step where the birth date is located)

Step 1: Look up Principal Qi fixed table to determine the Principal Qi of that qi-step
Step 2: Principal Qi = table lookup result

Output: Principal Qi Six Qi
```

**Principal Qi six steps fixed table** (review):

| Qi Step | Principal Qi | Starting Solar Term | Ending Solar Term | Approx. Days |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| First Qi | Jueyin Wind Wood | Great Cold | Awakening of Insects | ~60 days |
| Second Qi | Shaoyin Sovereign Fire | Spring Equinox | Beginning of Summer | ~60 days |
| Third Qi | Shaoyang Ministerial Fire | Grain Buds | Minor Heat | ~60 days |
| Fourth Qi | Taiyin Damp Earth | Major Heat | White Dew | ~60 days |
| Fifth Qi | Yangming Dry Metal | Autumn Equinox | Beginning of Winter | ~60 days |
| Final Qi | Taiyang Cold Water | Minor Snow | Minor Cold | ~60 days |

### 5.11 Calculation Method of Guest Qi

**Definition**: Guest Qi is the changing six-step qi each year, calculated from Sovereign Heaven (Third Qi), arranged according to the Three Yin Three Yang cyclic order.

**Calculation formula**:
```
Input: Sovereign Heaven Six Qi (determined by Year Branch)

Step 1: Guest Qi Third Qi = Sovereign Heaven Six Qi
Step 2: According to Three Yin Three Yang cyclic order (Jueyin→Shaoyin→Taiyin→Shaoyang→Yangming→Taiyang), from Third Qi push forward 2 steps to get First Qi, Second Qi, push backward 3 steps to get Fourth Qi, Fifth Qi, Final Qi
Step 3: Six steps of Guest Qi = calculation result

Output: Six steps of Guest Qi (First to Final)
```

### 5.12 Calculation Example: 1990 (Geng Wu year, Wu year→Sovereign Heaven=Shaoyin Sovereign Fire)

**Principal Qi** (fixed):
- First Qi: Jueyin Wind Wood
- Second Qi: Shaoyin Sovereign Fire
- Third Qi: Shaoyang Ministerial Fire
- Fourth Qi: Taiyin Damp Earth
- Fifth Qi: Yangming Dry Metal
- Final Qi: Taiyang Cold Water

**Guest Qi** (calculated from Sovereign Heaven=Shaoyin Sovereign Fire):
1. Third Qi = Shaoyin Sovereign Fire (Sovereign Heaven)
2. Push forward:
   - Second Qi = Jueyin Wind Wood (Shaoyin push forward 1 step)
   - First Qi = Taiyang Cold Water (Shaoyin push forward 2 steps)
3. Push backward:
   - Fourth Qi = Taiyin Damp Earth (Shaoyin push backward 1 step)
   - Fifth Qi = Shaoyang Ministerial Fire (Shaoyin push backward 2 steps)
   - Final Qi = Yangming Dry Metal (Shaoyin push backward 3 steps, i.e., Earthly Deputy)
4. Six steps of Guest Qi:
   - First Qi: Taiyang Cold Water
   - Second Qi: Jueyin Wind Wood
   - Third Qi: Shaoyin Sovereign Fire (Sovereign Heaven)
   - Fourth Qi: Taiyin Damp Earth
   - Fifth Qi: Shaoyang Ministerial Fire
   - Final Qi: Yangming Dry Metal (Earthly Deputy)

**Guest-Principal Superimposition interpretation**:
- First Qi (Great Cold-Awakening of Insects): Principal Qi Jueyin Wind Wood + Guest Qi Taiyang Cold Water → Guest Qi generates Principal Qi (Water generates Wood), compatible, climate偏 cold, prone to colds
- Second Qi (Spring Equinox-Beginning of Summer): Principal Qi Shaoyin Sovereign Fire + Guest Qi Jueyin Wind Wood → Guest Qi generates Principal Qi (Wood generates Fire), compatible, climate偏 warm, prone to heat
- Third Qi (Grain Buds-Minor Heat): Principal Qi Shaoyang Ministerial Fire + Guest Qi Shaoyin Sovereign Fire → Guest-Principal same qi (both Fire), compatible but excessive, climate hot, prone to heat diseases
- Fourth Qi (Major Heat-White Dew): Principal Qi Taiyin Damp Earth + Guest Qi Taiyin Damp Earth → Guest-Principal same qi (both Damp Earth), compatible but excessive, climate humid, prone to damp diseases
- Fifth Qi (Autumn Equinox-Beginning of Winter): Principal Qi Yangming Dry Metal + Guest Qi Shaoyang Ministerial Fire → Guest Qi restricts Principal Qi (Fire restricts Metal), incompatible, climate偏 hot-dry, prone to cough
- Final Qi (Minor Snow-Minor Cold): Principal Qi Taiyang Cold Water + Guest Qi Yangming Dry Metal → Principal Qi generates Guest Qi (Water generates Metal), compatible, climate偏 cold-dry, prone to colds, dry skin

## 6. Judgment of Heavenly Concord and Annual Concord

Heavenly Concord, Annual Concord, Supreme Heavenly Concord, Same Heavenly Concord, and Same Annual Concord are five special year configurations, representing the "resonance" state of Five Motions and Six Qi, with higher degrees of climatic abnormality and disease severity than ordinary years.

### 5.16 Judgment of Heavenly Concord

**Definition**: Heavenly Concord is a year where Annual Motion Five Elements is the same as Sovereign Heaven Five Elements.

**Judgment formula**:
```
if Annual Motion Five Elements == Sovereign Heaven Five Elements:
    Heavenly Concord = yes
else:
    Heavenly Concord = no
```

### 5.17 Judgment of Annual Concord

**Definition**: Annual Concord is a year where Annual Motion Five Elements is the same as Year Branch Five Elements.

**Judgment formula**:
```
if Annual Motion Five Elements == Year Branch Five Elements:
    Annual Concord = yes
else:
    Annual Concord = no
```

### 5.18 Judgment of Supreme Heavenly Concord

**Definition**: Supreme Heavenly Concord is a year that is both Heavenly Concord and Annual Concord.

**Judgment formula**:
```
if Heavenly Concord == yes and Annual Concord == yes:
    Supreme Heavenly Concord = yes
else:
    Supreme Heavenly Concord = no
```

### 5.19 Judgment of Same Heavenly Concord and Same Annual Concord

**Same Heavenly Concord**: A year where Annual Motion is Excess and the same as Earthly Deputy Five Elements.
```
if Annual Motion Excess/Deficiency == Excess and Annual Motion Five Elements == Earthly Deputy Five Elements:
    Same Heavenly Concord = yes
else:
    Same Heavenly Concord = no
```

**Same Annual Concord**: A year where Annual Motion is Deficiency and the same as Earthly Deputy Five Elements.
```
if Annual Motion Excess/Deficiency == Deficiency and Annual Motion Five Elements == Earthly Deputy Five Elements:
    Same Annual Concord = yes
else:
    Same Annual Concord = no
```

## 7. Identification of Congenital Gap

Congenital Gap is an original concept proposed in this book, referring to the Five Elements/zang-fu organs that are suppressed and weak in the Five Motions and Six Qi configuration at birth, serving as an individual's lifelong constitutional weakness.

### 5.21 Identification Method of Congenital Gap

**Definition**: Congenital Gap is the Five Elements/zang-fu organs that are most severely suppressed in the Five Motions and Six Qi configuration at birth.

**Identification steps**:
```
Input: Annual Motion, Sovereign Heaven, Earthly Deputy, Principal Motion, Guest Motion, Principal Qi, Guest Qi of birth year

Step 1: Calculate the "suppression value" of each Five Elements
  - Suppression value = (sum of dynamic values of elements that restrict me) - (sum of dynamic values of elements that generate me)
  - The higher the suppression value, the more severely the Five Elements is suppressed
Step 2: Find the Five Elements with the highest suppression value
Step 3: The zang-fu organs corresponding to this Five Elements are the "Congenital Gap"
Step 4: Calculate gap degree = suppression value / average of all Five Elements suppression values

Output: Congenital Gap Five Elements + corresponding zang-fu organs + gap degree
```

### 5.22 Calculation Example: 1990 (Geng Wu year, Metal Motion Excess, Sovereign Heaven Shaoyin Sovereign Fire, Earthly Deputy Yangming Dry Metal)

**Dynamic values of each Five Elements**:
- Wood: Annual Motion does not involve Wood, Principal Motion First Motion Shao Jue (-6), Guest Motion Third Motion Tai Jue (+6) → Wood dynamic value = -6 + 6 = 0
- Fire: Sovereign Heaven Shaoyin Sovereign Fire (+10, Zheng Hua), Principal Motion Second Motion Tai Zhi (+6), Guest Motion Fourth Motion Shao Zhi (-6) → Fire dynamic value = 10 + 6 - 6 = 10
- Earth: Principal Motion Third Motion Shao Gong (-10), Guest Motion Final Motion Tai Gong (+10) → Earth dynamic value = -10 + 10 = 0
- Metal: Annual Motion Tai Shang (+8), Earthly Deputy Yangming Dry Metal (+10, Zheng Hua), Principal Motion Fourth Motion Tai Shang (+8), Guest Motion First Motion Tai Shang (+8) → Metal dynamic value = 8 + 10 + 8 + 8 = 34
- Water: Principal Motion Final Motion Shao Yu (-8), Guest Motion Second Motion Shao Yu (-8) → Water dynamic value = -8 - 8 = -16

**Suppression values of each Five Elements** (suppression value=sum of dynamic values of restrictors - sum of dynamic values of generators):
- Wood: restrictor=Metal (34), generator=Water (-16) → suppression value = 34 - (-16) = 50
- Fire: restrictor=Water (-16), generator=Wood (0) → suppression value = -16 - 0 = -16 (negative value means not suppressed,反而 excessive)
- Earth: restrictor=Wood (0), generator=Fire (10) → suppression value = 0 - 10 = -10 (negative value means not suppressed)
- Metal: restrictor=Fire (10), generator=Earth (0) → suppression value = 10 - 0 = 10
- Water: restrictor=Earth (0), generator=Metal (34) → suppression value = 0 - 34 = -34 (negative value means not suppressed,反而 excessive)

**Congenital Gap identification**:
- Five Elements with highest suppression value = Wood (suppression value=50)

## 8. Relationship Between Congenital Endowment and Constitution

### 5.23 "Three-Dimensional Model" of Congenital Endowment Determining Constitution

This book proposes that the impact of congenital endowment on constitution can be described by a "three-dimensional model":

**First dimension: Annual Motion dimension (overall excess-deficiency)**
- Annual Motion determines the overall Five Elements tone of the birth year, which element is excessive and which is deficient.
- This dimension determines the "general direction" of constitution — whether it tends toward heat or cold, whether it tends toward excess or deficiency.

**Second dimension: Sovereign Heaven-Earthly Deputy dimension (first-second half year differences)**
- Sovereign Heaven determines the Six Qi tone of the first half of the year, Earthly Deputy determines the Six Qi tone of the second half of the year.
- This dimension determines the "temporal differences" of constitution — what diseases are prone to occur in the first half of the year, what diseases are prone to occur in the second half of the year.

**Third dimension: Principal-Guest Motion and Guest-Principal Superimposition dimension (inter-seasonal differences)**
- The superimposition of Principal Motion/Guest Motion and Principal Qi/Guest Qi determines the distribution of Five Elements and Six Qi in different seasons within a year.
- This dimension determines the "seasonal differences" of constitution — which season is prone to disease onset, which season is prone to exacerbation.

These three dimensions act jointly to determine an individual's complete constitutional characteristics.

## 9. Falsifiability of Congenital Endowment

### 5.26 Falsifiable Predictions

Congenital endowment theory is falsifiable because it can make clear, testable predictions:

**Prediction 1: Constitution prediction**
- Prediction content: People born in a certain Annual Motion year should have constitutional characteristics (such as cold-heat excess-deficiency, zang-fu strength-weakness) consistent with the excess-deficiency of Annual Motion Five Elements.
- Test method: Large-sample constitution survey, comparing the distribution of constitutional characteristics of populations born in different Annual Motion years.
- Falsification condition: If there is no statistically significant difference in the distribution of constitutional characteristics of populations born in different Annual Motion years, then Prediction 1 is falsified.

**Prediction 2: Disease susceptibility prediction**
- Prediction content: People born in a certain Annual Motion year should have higher incidence rates of certain diseases than people born in other years.
- Test method: Large-sample epidemiological survey, comparing disease incidence rates of populations born in different Annual Motion years.
- Falsification condition: If there is no statistically significant difference in disease incidence rates of populations born in different Annual Motion years, then Prediction 2 is falsified.

**Prediction 3: Disease onset time prediction**
- Prediction content: People with a certain Congenital Gap should have disease onset or exacerbation times consistent with the Five Motions and Six Qi cycle.
- Test method: Longitudinal cohort study, tracking and observing the disease onset time patterns of populations with different Congenital Gaps.
- Falsification condition: If there is no statistically significant difference in disease onset time patterns of populations with different Congenital Gaps, then Prediction 3 is falsified.

### 5.27 Possible Confounding Factors

When testing congenital endowment theory, the following confounding factors need to be controlled:

1. **Genetic factors**: Family genetic disease history, gene polymorphisms, etc., may affect constitution and disease susceptibility.
2. **Environmental factors**: Climate, geography, environmental pollution of birthplace and growth place, etc., may affect constitution.
3. **Lifestyle factors**: Diet, sleep, exercise, tobacco and alcohol, work stress, etc., may affect constitution and disease.
4. **Medical factors**: Vaccinations, medication use, surgical history, etc., may affect disease occurrence.
5. **Socioeconomic factors**: Income, education, occupation, etc., may affect health status and medical accessibility.

Only after controlling these confounding factors can the independent effect of congenital endowment be accurately tested.
