# Chapter 2: Tracing the Origins of Five Motions and Six Qi Theory in the Huangdi Neijing

## 1. Five Motions and Six Qi in the Neijing: The Seven Great Discourses

The theory of Five Motions and Six Qi is primarily concentrated in the "Seven Great Discourses" of the *Huangdi Neijing·Suwen* (Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon·Plain Questions). These seven discourses include: *Tianyuan Ji Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Origin of the Heavens), *Wu Yunxing Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Five Motions), *Liu Weizhi Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Subtle Principles of the Six), *Qi Jiaobian Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Transformations of Qi Intercourse), *Wu Changzheng Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Five Constant Governances), *Liu Yuanzheng Ji Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Six Primordial Standards), and *Zhizhenyao Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Ultimate Truth and Essentials).

Regarding the dating and authorship of these seven discourses, scholars throughout history have held controversies. It is generally believed that these seven discourses were not original content of the *Suwen*, but were added by Wang Bing during the Tang Dynasty when he organized the *Suwen*. Wang Bing stated in the *Preface to Suwen*: "At that time, in the study hall of Master Guo, I received a secret text from my predecessor Master Zhang, with clear characters and comprehensive principles; upon careful examination, all doubts were resolved." [To be verified: The original text of Wang Bing's preface needs to be checked] This indicates that the seven discourses may have been obtained by Wang Bing from a folk secret text and added to the *Suwen*.

Despite the controversy over their dating, the theory of Five Motions and Six Qi expounded in the seven discourses is undoubtedly the most systematic and complete chronomedicine theory in Traditional Chinese Medicine. Below is a梳理 of the core content of each discourse.

### 2.1 *Tianyuan Ji Da Lun*: The General Outline of Five Motions and Six Qi

*Tianyuan Ji Da Lun* is the first of the seven discourses and also the general outline of the Five Motions and Six Qi theory. This discourse mainly expounds the following core issues:

**First, the astronomical foundation of Five Motions and Six Qi.** The discourse states: "I have extensively examined the *Taishi Tianyuan Ce* (Primordial Heavenly Register), which says: The great void is vast and boundless, the foundation of transformation begins, all things rely on it for their beginning, the five motions terminate in heaven, qi is distributed to the true and spiritual, comprehensively governing the earth's origin, the nine stars hang brightly, the seven luminaries revolve, called yin and yang, called soft and hard, the dark and bright have their positions, cold and heat alternate, life begets life and transformation begets transformation, all things are manifest." [To be verified: The original text needs to be checked] This passage indicates that the theoretical foundation of Five Motions and Six Qi is astronomical observation — the movements of the Nine Stars (Tianpeng, Tianrui, Tianchong, Tianfu, Tianqin, Tianxin, Tianren, Tianying, Tianzhu) and the Seven Luminaries (Sun, Moon, Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, Earth) determine the yin-yang changes and cold-heat alternation between heaven and earth.

**Second, Heavenly Stems transforming into Motions and Earthly Branches transforming into Qi.** The discourse clarifies the correspondence between Heavenly Stems and Five Motions: "In years of Jia and Ji, Earth Motion governs; in years of Yi and Geng, Metal Motion governs; in years of Bing and Xin, Water Motion governs; in years of Ding and Ren, Wood Motion governs; in years of Wu and Gui, Fire Motion governs." It also clarifies the correspondence between Earthly Branches and Six Qi: "In years of Zi and Wu, Shaoyin appears above; in years of Chou and Wei, Taiyin appears above; in years of Yin and Shen, Shaoyang appears above; in years of Mao and You, Yangming appears above; in years of Chen and Xu, Taiyang appears above; in years of Si and Hai, Jueyin appears above." These two correspondences are the foundation of Five Motions and Six Qi computation.

**Third, excess and deficiency of Five Motions and Six Qi.** The discourse states: "Heaven uses six as its measure, earth uses five as its regulation. Circling heaven's qi, six periods make one completion; terminating earth's cycle, five years make one revolution. Sovereign fire uses brightness, ministerial fire uses position. Five and six combine, and 720 qi make one epoch, totaling thirty years; 1440 qi, totaling sixty years make one cycle, and deficiency and excess are all manifested." This passage indicates that the least common multiple of Five Motions (5-year cycle) and Six Qi (6-year cycle) is 30 years (one epoch), while 60 years (one cycle) is the complete cycle of Five Motions and Six Qi, in which both excess and deficiency appear.

### 2.2 *Wu Yunxing Da Lun*: The Movement Patterns of the Five Motions

*Wu Yunxing Da Lun* mainly expounds the movement patterns of the Five Motions and their correspondence with heaven-earth nature.

**First, the directions and Five Elements attributes of the Five Motions.** The discourse states: "The East generates wind, wind generates wood, wood generates sour, sour generates liver, liver generates sinews, sinews generate heart... The South generates heat, heat generates fire, fire generates bitter, bitter generates heart, heart generates blood, blood generates spleen... The Center generates dampness, dampness generates earth, earth generates sweet, sweet generates spleen, spleen generates flesh, flesh generates lung... The West generates dryness, dryness generates metal, metal generates pungent, pungent generates lung, lung generates skin and hair, skin and hair generate kidney... The North generates cold, cold generates water, water generates salty, salty generates kidney, kidney generates bone marrow, marrow generates liver." This passage establishes the correspondence among the Five Directions, Five Qi, Five Elements, Five Flavors, Five Zang, and Five Tissues, which is the foundation for linking Five Motions and Six Qi theory with human zang-fu organs.

**Second, the up-down-left-right positions of the Five Motions.** The discourse expounds the spatial positional relationships of the Five Motions: "The so-called Wu-Ji division is Kui, Bi, Jiao, Zhen, which are the gates of heaven and earth. This is where climatic signs begin and the Dao is generated, which cannot be ununderstood." [To be verified: The original text needs to be checked] This indicates that the movement of the Five Motions is related to astronomical coordinates (the Twenty-Eight Mansions), and the positions of the four mansions Kui, Bi, Jiao, and Zhen are the gates for the ascent and descent of heaven-earth qi.

**Third, the relationship between Five Motions and Six Qi.** The discourse states: "Dryness dries them, heat steams them, wind moves them, dampness moistens them, cold hardens them, fire warms them. Therefore wind-cold is below, dry-heat is above, damp qi is in the center, fire travels among them, cold and heat enter in six ways, thus causing emptiness and generating transformation." This passage describes the nature and positions of the Six Qi (wind, cold, summer-heat, dampness, dryness, fire) and their interactions with the Five Motions.

### 2.3 *Liu Weizhi Da Lun*: The Subtle Patterns of the Six Qi

*Liu Weizhi Da Lun* mainly expounds the subtle patterns of the Six Qi, including core concepts such as Principal Qi (Zhu Qi), Guest Qi (Ke Qi), Si Tian (Heavenly Sovereign), Zai Quan (Earthly Deputy), and Guest-Principal Superimposition (Ke Zhu Jia Lin).

**First, the fixed positions of Principal Qi.** The discourse states: "To the right of Xianming (Bright Manifestation) is the position of Sovereign Fire; to the right of Sovereign Fire, retreating one step, Ministerial Fire governs; advancing another step, Earth Qi governs; another step, Metal Qi governs; another step, Water Qi governs; another step, Wood Qi governs; another step, Sovereign Fire governs." This passage clarifies the fixed order of the six steps of Principal Qi: Jueyin Wind Wood (First Qi) → Shaoyin Sovereign Fire (Second Qi) → Shaoyang Ministerial Fire (Third Qi) → Taiyin Damp Earth (Fourth Qi) → Yangming Dry Metal (Fifth Qi) → Taiyang Cold Water (Final Qi).

**Second, Guest Qi's Si Tian and Zai Quan.** The discourse states: "Above and below have positions, left and right have order. Therefore to the right of Shaoyang, Yangming governs; to the right of Yangming, Taiyang governs; to the right of Taiyang, Jueyin governs; to the right of Jueyin, Shaoyin governs; to the right of Shaoyin, Taiyin governs; to the right of Taiyin, Shaoyang governs. This is called the branch of qi, facing south and awaiting it." This passage clarifies the排列 order of the six steps of Guest Qi and the positional relationship between Si Tian (Third Qi) and Zai Quan (Final Qi).

**Third, compatibility and incompatibility in Guest-Principal Superimposition.** The discourse states: "When qi is compatible, there is harmony; when incompatible, there is disease." It also states: "When the sovereign occupies the minister's position, it is顺 (favorable); when the minister occupies the sovereign's position, it is逆 (adverse). When adverse, the disease is near and the harm is swift; when favorable, the disease is distant and the harm is slight. This refers to the two fires." [To be verified: The original text needs to be checked] This passage expounds the judgment criteria for Guest-Principal Superimposition — when Guest Qi generates Principal Qi or Guest and Principal Qi are the same, it is "compatible" (harmonious climate); when Guest Qi restricts Principal Qi or Principal Qi restricts Guest Qi, it is "incompatible" (abnormal climate, prone to disease).

**Fourth, Tian Fu (Heavenly Concord), Sui Hui (Annual Concord), and Taiyi Tian Fu (Supreme Heavenly Concord).** The discourse clarifies the definitions of these three special years: "Tian Fu is when the Annual Motion and Si Tian are the same qi." "Sui Hui is when the Annual Motion and the year's branch are the same qi." "Taiyi Tian Fu is when Tian Fu and Sui Hui combine." It also explains the disease characteristics of these special years: "Tian Fu is like the law enforcer, Sui Hui is like the executive order, Taiyi Tian Fu is like the noble person. Those affected by the law enforcer have swift and dangerous disease; those affected by the executive order have slow and persistent disease; those affected by the noble person have sudden and fatal disease." ### 2.4 *Qi Jiaobian Da Lun*: Climatic Changes and Disease Effects of Five Motions Excess and Deficiency

*Qi Jiaobian Da Lun* mainly expounds the effects of Five Motions excess and deficiency on climatic changes and human diseases.

The core content of this discourse is to separately describe the climatic changes, phenological changes, and disease effects in years of excess and deficiency for each of the Five Motions (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water). For example:

"In years of Wood Motion excess, wind qi prevails, and spleen-earth is affected by evil. People suffer from diarrhea with undigested food, reduced appetite, heavy body, vexation and oppression, bowel sounds, abdominal distension, corresponding to the Year Star (Jupiter) above. In severe cases, there is sudden anger, dizziness and vertigo, head-top diseases. Transformation qi fails to govern, generation qi alone dominates, clouds and objects fly and move, plants and trees are restless, even shaking and falling, conversely flank pain and severe vomiting, and those with Chongyang pulse extinct die and cannot be treated." [To be verified: The original text needs to be checked]

"In years of Wood Motion deficiency, dryness prevails greatly, generation qi fails to respond, plants and trees bloom late, killing and withering is severe, then hard trees split and crack, soft ones wither and dry, corresponding to the White Star (Venus) above. People suffer from internal cold, flank pain, lower abdominal pain, bowel sounds, loose diarrhea, cool rains arrive timely, corresponding to the White Star above, and the grains are green." [To be verified: The original text needs to be checked]

Such detailed descriptions provide specific references for the clinical application of Five Motions and Six Qi. At the same time, the discourse also emphasizes the concept of "virtue-transformation-governance-order-disaster-change" — the changes of Five Motions have normal "virtue and transformation" and abnormal "disaster and change," and the occurrence of human diseases is related to the abnormal changes of Five Motions.

### 2.5 *Wu Changzheng Da Lun*: Balanced Qi, Excess, and Deficiency of the Five Motions

*Wu Changzheng Da Lun* mainly expounds the three states of the Five Motions: Balanced Qi (Ping Qi), Excess (Tai Guo), and Deficiency (Bu Ji), as well as the climatic changes, phenological changes, and disease effects in each state.

The core contribution of this discourse is proposing the concept of "Balanced Qi" and providing detailed descriptions of Balanced Qi, Excess, and Deficiency for each of the Five Motions. For example:

"Wood is called Spreading Harmony (Fu He), Fire is called Rising Brightness (Sheng Ming), Earth is called Complete Transformation (Bei Hua), Metal is called Judged Balance (Shen Ping), Water is called Quiet Obedience (Jing Shun)." — These are the names and characteristics of Balanced Qi for the Five Motions.

"Wood is called Generation (Fa Sheng), Fire is called Blazing Brilliance (He Xi), Earth is called Generous Mound (Dun Fu), Metal is called Firm Completion (Jian Cheng), Water is called Flowing Overflow (Liu Yan)." — These are the names and characteristics of Excess for the Five Motions.

"Wood is called Diminished Harmony (Wei He), Fire is called Hidden Brightness (Fu Ming), Earth is called Low Supervision (Bei Jian), Metal is called Following Reform (Cong Ge), Water is called Dried Flow (He Liu)." — These are the names and characteristics of Deficiency for the Five Motions.

For each state, the discourse describes in detail "virtue, transformation, governance, order, change, disaster" and the corresponding human diseases. This systematic classification provides a foundation for the refined calculation of Five Motions and Six Qi.

### 2.6 *Liu Yuanzheng Ji Da Lun*: Year-by-Year Calculation of the Six Qi

*Liu Yuanzheng Ji Da Lun* is the longest of the seven discourses. Its main content is the year-by-year calculation of the Five Motions and Six Qi configurations for sixty years, including each year's Annual Motion (Sui Yun), Si Tian, Zai Quan, six steps of Principal Qi, six steps of Guest Qi, climatic changes, phenological changes, and disease effects.

The core value of this discourse lies in: it provides a complete Five Motions and Six Qi calculation table for sixty years, serving as the "perpetual calendar" for the clinical application of Five Motions and Six Qi. At the same time, the discourse also expounds in detail the concept of "Stagnation-Eruption" (Yu Fa) — the occurrence conditions, eruption times, and clinical manifestations of the Five Stagnations (Wood Stagnation, Fire Stagnation, Earth Stagnation, Metal Stagnation, Water Stagnation), as well as the "Five Stagnations Therapeutic Principles" (Wood Stagnation: diffuse it; Fire Stagnation: erupt it; Earth Stagnation:夺 it; Metal Stagnation: drain it; Water Stagnation:折 it).

### 2.7 *Zhizhenyao Da Lun*: Disease Mechanisms and Therapeutic Principles of the Six Qi

*Zhizhenyao Da Lun* is the last of the seven discourses, mainly expounding the disease mechanisms and therapeutic principles of the Six Qi.

The core content of this discourse includes:

**First, the Nineteen Disease Mechanisms (Bing Ji Shi Jiu Tiao).** The discourse proposes the famous "Nineteen Disease Mechanisms,"归纳 various disease symptoms into five-zang disease mechanisms and six-qi disease mechanisms. For example: "All wind with shaking and dizziness belongs to the liver; all cold with contraction and pulling belongs to the kidney; all qi with congestion and oppression belongs to the lung; all dampness with swelling and fullness belongs to the spleen; all heat with clouding and convulsions belongs to fire..." **Second, therapeutic principles for Six Qi Si Tian and Zai Quan.** The discourse expounds in detail the therapeutic principles when each of the Six Qi is Si Tian or Zai Quan, including the selection of medicinal flavors and properties and compatibility methods. For example: "When Jueyin is Si Tian and wind excess prevails, balance it with pungent-cool, assist with bitter-sweet, relax it with sweet, drain it with sour." [To be verified: The original text needs to be checked] These therapeutic principles provide specific guidance for the clinical treatment of Five Motions and Six Qi.

**Third, Branch-Root-Medial Qi (Biao Ben Zhong Qi).** The discourse proposes the concept of "Branch-Root-Medial Qi," dividing the Six Channels (Three Yin and Three Yang) into three categories: Branch, Root, and Medial Qi, and expounding the therapeutic principles of "following the root," "following the branch," and "following the medial qi." This concept provides a theoretical foundation for combining Six-Channel syndrome differentiation with Five Motions and Six Qi.

## 2. Later Physicians' Interpretations and Developments of Five Motions and Six Qi

The seven discourses laid the theoretical foundation for Five Motions and Six Qi, but later physicians continuously interpreted and developed this theory in clinical practice. Below is a梳理 of several important historical periods and representative physicians.

### 2.8 Song Dynasty: Officialization of Five Motions and Six Qi

The Song Dynasty was an important period for the development of Five Motions and Six Qi theory. The Northern Song government established the "Imperial Medical Bureau" (Tai Yi Ju), listing Five Motions and Six Qi as required content in medical education. At the same time, several important monographs on Five Motions and Six Qi appeared in the Song Dynasty.

**Liu Wenshu's *Suwen Rushi Yunqi Lun Ao* (Essentials of Motion-Qi Theory for Entering the Plain Questions):** This is one of the earliest surviving monographs on Five Motions and Six Qi, completed in the second year of Yuanfu of the Northern Song Dynasty (1099). Liu Wenshu systematically梳理 the basic concepts and calculation methods of Five Motions and Six Qi in the book, and drew a large number of charts, making the calculation of Five Motions and Six Qi more intuitive. This book had a great influence on later generations and became an introductory text for learning Five Motions and Six Qi.

**Shi Song's collation of *Huangdi Neijing Suwen*:** During the Northern Song Dynasty, Shi Song systematically collated the *Huangdi Neijing Suwen*, making the text of the seven discourses more accurate and laying the foundation for later research.

The officialization of Five Motions and Six Qi in the Song Dynasty made this theory essential knowledge for physicians, but it also brought some problems — some physicians mechanically applied Five Motions and Six Qi, ignoring the importance of clinical syndrome differentiation, leading to a tendency of "Five Motions and Six Qi omnipotence."

### 2.9 Jin-Yuan Period: Integration of Five Motions and Six Qi with Clinical Practice

The Jin-Yuan period was the golden age of TCM development and also a period of deep integration between Five Motions and Six Qi and clinical practice. Representative physicians of this period include Liu Wansu, Li Dongyuan, Zhu Danxi, etc., who all applied Five Motions and Six Qi theory to varying degrees.

**Liu Wansu (Cold-Cool School):** Liu Wansu was deeply influenced by Five Motions and Six Qi theory. In his *Suwen Xuanji Yuanbing Shi* (Original Disease Patterns from the Mystery of the Plain Questions), he combined the "Nineteen Disease Mechanisms" with Five Motions and Six Qi, proposing the core viewpoint of "Fire-Heat Theory." He believed that diseases at that time were mostly related to "fire-heat," and treatment should focus on cold-cool methods. Although Liu Wansu's "Fire-Heat Theory" was not directly derived from Five Motions and Six Qi, his methodology — combining six-qi disease mechanisms with clinical syndrome differentiation — was deeply influenced by Five Motions and Six Qi theory.

**Li Dongyuan (Earth-Supplementing School):** Li Dongyuan proposed the core viewpoint of "internal damage to the spleen and stomach gives rise to all diseases" in his *Pi Wei Lun* (Treatise on the Spleen and Stomach). Although his theory centered on the spleen and stomach, he also applied the thinking mode of Five Motions and Six Qi — emphasizing the qi movement of "ascending-descending-floating-sinking," which is consistent with the Five Motions pattern of "generation-growth-transformation-collection-storage" in Five Motions and Six Qi.

**Zhu Danxi (Yin-Nourishing School):** Zhu Danxi proposed the viewpoint of "yang is often excess, yin is often deficiency," with treatment focusing on nourishing yin. His theory was also influenced by Five Motions and Six Qi — he believed that "ministerial fire" is the driving force of human life activities, but excessive ministerial fire burns yin essence, which has similarities with the concept of "Shaoyang Ministerial Fire" in Five Motions and Six Qi.

Although the theories of the Four Great Masters of the Jin-Yuan period differed, they all applied the thinking mode of Five Motions and Six Qi to varying degrees — linking the change patterns of heaven-earth nature with human physiology and pathology. This indicates that Five Motions and Six Qi theory had developed from "calculating climate" to a practical tool for "guiding clinical practice."

### 2.10 Ming-Qing Period: Systematization and Questioning of Five Motions and Six Qi

The Ming-Qing period was a period of systematization for Five Motions and Six Qi theory, and also a period when questioning of this theory began to emerge.

**Wang Ji's *Yunqi Yi Lan* (Easy Overview of Motion-Qi):** In the Ming Dynasty, Wang Ji systematically梳理 the calculation methods of Five Motions and Six Qi in *Yunqi Yi Lan*, and explained them in combination with clinical cases. The characteristic of this book is "explaining the profound in simple terms," simplifying the complex calculation of Five Motions and Six Qi into easy-to-master steps, which is very helpful for beginners.

**Zhang Jingyue's *Lei Jing* (Classified Canon):** In the Ming Dynasty, Zhang Jingyue classified and compiled the *Huangdi Neijing* in *Lei Jing*, in which the "Motion-Qi Category" specifically收录 the content of the seven discourses and provided detailed annotations. Zhang Jingyue's annotations were rigorous and systematic, serving as an important reference for later research on Five Motions and Six Qi. At the same time, Zhang Jingyue also applied Five Motions and Six Qi theory in *Jingyue Quanshu* (Complete Works of Jingyue), proposing the viewpoint of "yang is not necessarily excess, yin is often deficiency," which contrasted with Zhu Danxi's "yang is often excess, yin is often deficiency."

**Wu Tang's *Wen Bing Tiao Bian* (Systematic Differentiation of Warm Diseases):** In the Qing Dynasty, Wu Tang founded "Triple-Jiao Syndrome Differentiation" in *Wen Bing Tiao Bian*, but he also applied the thinking mode of Five Motions and Six Qi — linking the occurrence of warm diseases with seasonal climate, proposing the classification method of "Four-Season Warm Diseases." This classification method is consistent with the seasonal changes of the Six Qi in Five Motions and Six Qi.

**Wang Qingren's *Yi Lin Gai Cuo* (Corrections of Medical Errors):** In the Qing Dynasty, Wang Qingren questioned the traditional TCM zang-fu theory in *Yi Lin Gai Cuo*, emphasizing the importance of anatomical observation. Although he did not directly question Five Motions and Six Qi, his empirical spirit had a profound influence on later generations — some physicians began to examine Five Motions and Six Qi theory with an empirical eye, questioning the accuracy of its calculations and its clinical value.

During the Ming-Qing period, while Five Motions and Six Qi theory was being systematized, it also began to be questioned. This questioning mainly focused on two aspects: First, is the calculation of Five Motions and Six Qi accurate? Second, is Five Motions and Six Qi really useful in clinical practice? These questions promoted the reflection and correction of Five Motions and Six Qi theory.

### 2.11 Modern and Contemporary Period: Marginalization and Renewed Attention to Five Motions and Six Qi

Since modern times, with the introduction and development of Western medicine, TCM as a whole has been impacted, and Five Motions and Six Qi theory has been even more marginalized. There are three main reasons:

**First, the calculation of Five Motions and Six Qi is complex and difficult to master.** In the modern medical education system, Five Motions and Six Qi is usually only part of the *Huangdi Neijing* course, with limited class hours, making it difficult for students to master systematically.

**Second, Five Motions and Six Qi lacks empirical verification.** In the context of modern science, Five Motions and Six Qi theory is considered "empirical" or "philosophical," lacking rigorous empirical verification, and therefore difficult to be accepted by the mainstream medical community.

**Third, the clinical application value of Five Motions and Six Qi is unclear.** Although physicians throughout history have applied Five Motions and Six Qi, there is a lack of large-sample clinical studies to prove its effectiveness, so its clinical application value has always been controversial.

However, in recent decades, with the development of disciplines such as chronobiology, climate-health science, and complex systems science, Five Motions and Six Qi theory has received renewed attention. Some scholars have begun to study Five Motions and Six Qi using modern scientific methods, including:

- Using statistical methods to analyze the correlation between Five Motions and Six Qi calculations and actual climate and disease;
- Using chronobiological methods to study human seasonal and annual rhythms;
- Using complex systems science methods to model the computational logic of Five Motions and Six Qi;
- Using machine learning methods to predict seasonal disease outbreaks.

Although these studies are still in the preliminary stage, they provide new ideas and methods for the modernization of Five Motions and Six Qi. The work of this book is precisely carried out against this background — attempting to organize Five Motions and Six Qi theory into a clear, computable, falsifiable algorithm model, providing a foundation for subsequent empirical verification.

## 3. Core Controversies in Five Motions and Six Qi Theory

After梳理 the origins of Five Motions and Six Qi theory, we must objectively present the core controversies that have existed in the history of this theory. These controversies are not only academic issues but also relate to how we understand and apply this theory.

### 2.12 Controversy One: Is Five Motions and Six Qi "Science" or "Metaphysics"?

This is the most fundamental controversy. Supporters believe that Five Motions and Six Qi is a pattern summarized by the ancients through long-term astronomical observation and climatic records, with an empirical foundation, and is "ancient chronomedicine." Questioners believe that the computational logic of Five Motions and Six Qi is too complex and far-fetched, lacking rigorous empirical verification, and is more like a "philosophical system" or "metaphysics" rather than science.

The position of this book is: Five Motions and Six Qi is a **falsifiable scientific conjecture**. It may be right or wrong, but it can be tested — through large-sample climate data and disease data, we can test whether there is a statistically significant correlation between Five Motions and Six Qi calculations and actual climate and disease. Before the test results are available, we should neither blindly believe nor blindly deny.

### 2.13 Controversy Two: Can Five Motions and Six Qi Predict Individual Diseases?

Five Motions and Six Qi calculates climatic and disease trends at the "population level," but disease occurrence is an "individual-level" event. In the same year, some people get sick and some do not; some get this disease and some get that disease. Questioners believe that Five Motions and Six Qi cannot explain such individual differences and therefore cannot be used for individual disease prediction.

The position of this book is: Five Motions and Six Qi **cannot independently predict individual diseases**, but can serve as **a temporal variable in a multifactorial causal model**, analyzed comprehensively together with other factors (genetics, lifestyle, psychological state, etc.). In this model, the results calculated by Five Motions and Six Qi are "risk alerts," not "diagnostic conclusions." At the same time, the "congenital endowment" calculation proposed in this book (Five Motions and Six Qi configuration at birth) provides a possible direction for explaining individual differences — different birth times may correspond to different constitutional weaknesses and disease susceptibility tendencies. Of course, this direction still requires extensive empirical verification.

### 2.14 Controversy Three: Is the Computational Standard of Five Motions and Six Qi Unified?

Physicians throughout history have held differences in the calculation methods of Five Motions and Six Qi, including: the starting time of Annual Motion (Beginning of Spring or Great Cold), the排列 order of Principal and Guest Qi, the judgment criteria for Guest-Principal Superimposition, the definitions of Tian Fu and Sui Hui, etc. These differences lead to inconsistent calculation results for Five Motions and Six Qi, affecting its clinical application.

The position of this book is: **a unified computational standard must be established.** When organizing the Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm, this book takes the seven discourses of the *Huangdi Neijing* as the foundation, refers to the mainstream viewpoints of physicians throughout history, and clarifies the standards and rules for each step of calculation. For areas where controversy exists, it is explicitly marked as "this book adopts XX standard" with reasons explained. The purpose of this is to make the calculation of Five Motions and Six Qi repeatable and testable — only when the computational standard is unified can the results of different researchers be compared and empirical verification be possible.

### 2.15 Controversy Four: Has Five Motions and Six Qi Become Obsolete?

Questioners believe that in today's highly developed modern weather forecasting and modern medicine, the ancient calculation method of Five Motions and Six Qi has become obsolete and has no practical application value.

The position of this book is: the **specific calculation methods of Five Motions and Six Qi may need correction and improvement**, but its **core idea — that human health is influenced by the periodic changes of heaven-earth nature — has not become obsolete**. Chronobiology has confirmed that the human body has multiple biological rhythms, and climate-health science has confirmed the impact of climate change on disease. Five Motions and Six Qi theory can be regarded as an "ancient chronomedicine model," whose core idea is consistent with modern chronobiology and climate-health science. What we need to do is not abandon this theory, but use modern scientific methods to verify, correct, and improve it.
