# Chapter 1: Preface — The Past, Present, and Future of Chronomedicine

## 1. Writing Background: An Ancient Discipline Set Aside

The *Huangdi Neijing* (Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon), compiled over two thousand years ago, is the foundational work of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM). In this classic, the ancients, through their unique observational methods and logical reasoning, constructed a complete theoretical system about the relationship between heaven-earth nature and human health — **Wu Yun Liu Qi (Five Motions and Six Qi)**.

The theory of Five Motions and Six Qi uses **Tian Gan (Heavenly Stems)** and **Di Zhi (Earthly Branches)** as basic computational units. Through the generation, restriction, and transformation of **Wu Xing (Five Elements)** and **Liu Qi (Six Qi)**, it calculates the patterns of climatic change for each year and each qi-step, as well as the impact of these changes on human health. This theory is systematically expounded in the "Seven Great Discourses" of the *Suwen* (Plain Questions), including *Tianyuan Ji Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Origin of the Heavens), *Wu Yunxing Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Five Motions), *Liu Weizhi Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Subtle Principles of the Six), *Qi Jiaobian Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Transformations of Qi Intercourse), *Wu Changzheng Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Five Constant Governances), *Liu Yuanzheng Ji Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Six Primordial Standards), and *Zhizhenyao Da Lun* (Great Discourse on the Ultimate Truth and Essentials).

However, with the development of TCM, the theory of Five Motions and Six Qi was gradually marginalized. The reasons are manifold: First, the computational logic of this theory is complex and the concepts are abstract, making it difficult for beginners to master. Second, physicians throughout history have held divergent understandings of Five Motions and Six Qi, lacking a unified computational standard. Third, and most critically — this theory lacks verification methods at the micro-level. The ancients could only corroborate it through macroscopic climatic observation and clinical experience, unable to establish repeatable, falsifiable mathematical models at the individual level.

As a result, Five Motions and Six Qi gradually became a kind of "classical philosophy" or "cultural heritage," rather than a practically applicable "chronomedicine algorithm." Many TCM practitioners know of this theory's existence but rarely use it in clinical practice; even more people regard it as "metaphysics" or "feudal superstition," keeping it at a respectful distance.

## 2. Core Question: Why Has Chronomedicine Stagnated?

Before delving deeply into Five Motions and Six Qi, we must first answer a fundamental question: Why has such a seemingly logically rigorous theory not seen substantive development over two thousand years?

The author believes the fundamental reason lies in **the lack of micro-level verification methods**.

The development of any scientific theory requires three stages: observe phenomena → build models → verify models. Through long-term astronomical observation and climatic records, the ancients established the theoretical model of Five Motions and Six Qi (observe phenomena → build models), but at the "verify models" stage, the ancients encountered insurmountable obstacles.

The ancients' methods of verifying Five Motions and Six Qi were primarily at the macroscopic level: observing whether a given year's climate matched the calculations, observing whether the incidence of certain diseases in a given year matched the calculations. This verification method has three fatal flaws:

**First, the sample size of macroscopic observation is too small.** A person can observe only a limited number of years in a lifetime and record only a limited number of cases, making large-sample statistical analysis impossible.

**Second, macroscopic observation has too many confounding factors.** The factors affecting climate and disease are extremely complex. In addition to the astronomical factors calculated by Five Motions and Six Qi, there are geographical factors, human factors, random factors, and more. The ancients could not control these confounding factors, and therefore could not determine whether the observed phenomena were truly caused by Five Motions and Six Qi.

**Third, macroscopic observation cannot be verified at the individual level.** Five Motions and Six Qi calculates climatic and disease trends at the "population level," but disease occurrence is an "individual-level" event. In the same year, some people get sick and some do not; some get this disease and some get that disease. The ancients could not explain such individual differences, and therefore could not establish predictive models at the individual level.

Precisely because of these three flaws, the theory of Five Motions and Six Qi has remained at the level of "macroscopic experience" for over two thousand years, unable to enter the realm of "microscopic verification." Without microscopic verification, a theory cannot be falsified, cannot be corrected, cannot be refined — and so it can only exist as a kind of "classical philosophy," rather than becoming a "science."

## 3. Historical Opportunity: The Possibility of Microscopic Verification Brought by Technological Development

Entering the 21st century, the development of human technology has provided unprecedented possibilities for the microscopic verification of Five Motions and Six Qi.

**First, the leap in computing power** has made large-scale data processing possible. We can collect decades or even centuries of climatic and disease data, and use statistical methods to analyze the correlation between Five Motions and Six Qi calculations and actual climate and disease. This was unimaginable in the ancients' era.

**Second, the development of molecular biology** allows us to observe changes in the human body at micro-levels such as gene expression, proteomics, and metabolomics. If the "excess of a certain qi in a certain year" calculated by Five Motions and Six Qi truly affects the human body, then this effect should be reflected in micro-level indicators such as gene expression, hormone levels, and immune function. We can design experiments to detect micro-level indicators in the same group of people during different years and different solar terms, to see whether they match the calculations of Five Motions and Six Qi.

**Third, the development of Chronobiology** has confirmed that the human body has multiple biological rhythms — circadian rhythms, lunar rhythms, seasonal rhythms, annual rhythms, and more. The existence of these rhythms indicates that the human body indeed has some correspondence with the temporal cycles of heaven-earth nature. The theory of Five Motions and Six Qi can be regarded as a kind of "ancient chronobiology" — it uses the computational methods of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches to describe the impact of heaven-earth nature's temporal cycles on the human body.

**Finally, the development of artificial intelligence and machine learning** has provided new tools for complex system modeling. Five Motions and Six Qi involves complex interactions among multiple variables (Sui Yun [Annual Motion], Zhu Yun [Principal Motion], Ke Yun [Guest Motion], Zhu Qi [Principal Qi], Ke Qi [Guest Qi], Si Tian [Heavenly Sovereign], Zai Quan [Earthly Deputy], etc.), and traditional statistical methods struggle to handle such high-dimensional nonlinear relationships. Machine learning models (such as neural networks, random forests, etc.) can automatically learn the complex relationships among these variables, thereby establishing more accurate predictive models.

It is precisely these technological developments that make it possible for the theory of Five Motions and Six Qi to move from "macroscopic experience" to "microscopic verification." This is an opportunity never seen in over two thousand years.

## 4. Core Thesis of This Book: Chronomedicine Under System Control Logic

Against this historical backdrop, this book proposes a core thesis: **If the world operates under a system control logic, then what Five Motions and Six Qi describes is the control law of the large system of heaven-earth nature; and the human body, as a subsystem of this large system, inevitably has its health state influenced by the control law of the large system.**

This thesis contains three layers of meaning:

**First, the world is system-controlled.** From the operation of the universe to climatic changes, from ecosystem succession to human physiological activities, all follow some kind of system control law. This law is not a mysterious "will of heaven," but an objective existence that can be observed, described, and modeled. What the ancients called the "Great Dao" (Da Dao), in modern language, is "system control law."

**Second, Five Motions and Six Qi is the ancients' description of the heaven-earth system's control law.** Through long-term astronomical observation and climatic records, the ancients discovered that the operation of heaven-earth nature follows certain periodic patterns — a major cycle of 60 years, a medium cycle of one year, and a minor cycle of each qi-step (approximately 60 days). The ancients used the computational methods of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches to encode these periodic patterns into the theory of Five Motions and Six Qi. This theory is essentially the ancients' "mathematical modeling" of the heaven-earth system's control law — although this modeling method differs from modern mathematics, its core logic is consistent: using finite variables and computational rules to describe the dynamic changes of complex systems.

**Third, the human body is a subsystem of the heaven-earth system, and its health state is influenced by the heaven-earth system's control law.** The ancients said, "Humans are born from the qi of heaven and earth, and mature according to the laws of the four seasons" (*Suwen·Baoming Quanxing Lun*), and also said, "Humans correspond with heaven and earth, and respond with the sun and moon" (*Lingshu·Suilu Lun*). In modern systems theory language, the human body is an open complex system that continuously exchanges matter, energy, and information with the external environment. Therefore, changes in the external environment (especially periodic climatic changes) inevitably affect the physiological state of the human body. What Five Motions and Six Qi calculates is precisely this periodic change in the external environment and its possible impact on human health.

Based on this core thesis, this book argues that Five Motions and Six Qi is not "metaphysics," nor "feudal superstition," but a "chronomedicine model" established by the ancients regarding the control law of the heaven-earth system. Although this model is ancient, crude, and lacking microscopic verification, its core logic — that human health is influenced by the periodic changes of heaven-earth nature — is consistent with the basic principles of modern systems theory and chronobiology.

## 5. Methodology: The Temporal Factor in a Multifactorial Causal Model

While proposing the core thesis, we must maintain a clear mind: **Chronomedicine (including Five Motions and Six Qi) is only one of many factors affecting human health, not the only factor.**

The occurrence of disease is an extremely complex process involving the interaction of multiple factors. These factors include:

- **Congenital factors**: genetic genes, Five Motions and Six Qi configuration at birth (congenital endowment), prenatal environment, etc.
- **Temporal factors**: current Five Motions and Six Qi configuration, Five Motions and Six Qi configuration at disease onset, circadian rhythms, seasonal rhythms, etc.
- **Environmental factors**: climate, geography, air quality, water quality, noise, radiation, etc.
- **Lifestyle factors**: diet, sleep, exercise, work, tobacco and alcohol, sexual activity, etc.
- **Psychological factors**: emotions, stress, personality, cognitive patterns, social relationships, etc.
- **Medical factors**: past medical history, medication history, surgical history, vaccinations, etc.
- **Random factors**: accidental exposure, gene mutations, random events, etc.

Among these factors, the temporal factor (described by Five Motions and Six Qi) is only one. It may increase or decrease the probability of a certain disease occurring, but it is usually not the decisive factor. In the same year and the same solar term, some people get sick and some do not — this is because other factors (genetics, lifestyle, psychological state, etc.) are at play.

Therefore, this book advocates establishing a **multifactorial causal model**, treating the temporal factor as one variable among others for comprehensive analysis. In this model, the results calculated by Five Motions and Six Qi are not "diagnostic conclusions," but "risk alerts" — they indicate that during a certain time period, the probability of a certain disease may increase or decrease, but whether the disease ultimately occurs depends on the combined action of other factors.

This methodological positioning avoids both the误区 of "Five Motions and Six Qi omnipotence" (believing that Five Motions and Six Qi can predict all diseases) and the误区 of "Five Motions and Six Qi uselessness" (believing that Five Motions and Six Qi has no value). It places Five Motions and Six Qi in an appropriate position: as a temporal variable in a multifactorial causal model, providing reference for disease prediction and prevention.

## 6. Positioning of This Book: A Falsifiable Conjecture, Not Ultimate Truth

After clarifying the core thesis and methodology, we must also clarify the positioning of this book: **The Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm model proposed in this book is a falsifiable conjecture, not ultimate truth.**

Any scientific theory must be falsifiable — that is, it must be capable of being refuted by observation or experiment. If a theory cannot be falsified (no matter what phenomena are observed, they can all be interpreted as "consistent with the theory"), then it is not science, but metaphysics or religion.

The Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm model proposed in this book is falsifiable. Its falsifiability is reflected in the following aspects:

**First, it can predict the climatic characteristics of a certain qi in a certain year.** If the actual climate of a given year seriously deviates from the calculation, and this deviation is statistically significant, then this part of the model is falsified.

**Second, it can predict the incidence trend of a certain disease in a certain year.** If the actual incidence of a certain disease in a given year is opposite to the calculated trend, and this opposition is statistically significant, then this part of the model is falsified.

**Third, it can predict the disease risk of an individual during a certain time period.** If large-sample cohort studies find no statistically significant difference in incidence between the high-risk group and the low-risk group, then the individual predictive capability of this model is falsified.

**Fourth, it can predict an individual's "congenital gap" (i.e., constitutional weakness).** If large-sample studies find no correlation between the calculated congenital gap and actual constitutional characteristics or disease susceptibility, then the congenital endowment calculation of this model is falsified.

Precisely because it is falsifiable, it is scientific — even if it is ultimately proven wrong, it provides a testable starting point for subsequent research. As Popper said: "The hallmark of a scientific theory is not that it can be proven right, but that it can be proven wrong."

Therefore, this book does not advocate that "Five Motions and Six Qi is absolute truth," nor does it advocate that "the wisdom of the ancients surpasses modern science." What this book advocates is: Five Motions and Six Qi is a scientific conjecture worthy of serious examination. It may be right, it may be wrong, or it may be partially right and partially wrong — but regardless of the outcome, the process of examining it itself will deepen our understanding of the relationship between human health and heaven-earth nature.

## 7. Value of This Book: Providing an Algorithmic Foundation for Chronomedicine in the AI Era

Finally, we must answer a question: In this era of highly developed technology, what is the value of writing such a book and creating such an algorithm model?

The author believes that the value of this book is mainly reflected in the following three aspects:

**First, providing an operable algorithmic framework for the microscopic verification of chronomedicine.** As mentioned earlier, the fundamental reason for the stagnation of Five Motions and Six Qi theory is the lack of microscopic verification methods. And the prerequisite for microscopic verification is having a clear, operable algorithm model — only when the model is clear can experiments be designed to test it. The core work of this book is to organize the scattered, vague theory of Five Motions and Six Qi in the *Huangdi Neijing* into a clear, computable algorithm model. This model may be imperfect and may contain errors, but it provides an operable starting point for subsequent microscopic verification.

**Second, providing a quantification scheme for the temporal factor in a multifactorial causal model.** Modern medicine increasingly emphasizes "precision medicine" and "personalized medicine," and the core of personalized medicine is establishing multifactorial causal models that comprehensively consider genetics, environment, lifestyle, time, and other factors to provide personalized health management plans for each individual. In this multifactorial model, the quantification of temporal factors (seasons, solar terms, annual motions, etc.) has long been a difficult problem. The Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm model proposed in this book provides a feasible scheme for the quantification of temporal factors — although it still requires extensive verification and correction.

**Third, providing an alternative "chronomedicine algorithm" for health guardianship in the era of artificial intelligence.** With the development of artificial intelligence technology, more and more AI health assistants, AI diagnostic systems, and AI health management platforms are emerging. These AI systems require various algorithms for support, among which the "chronomedicine algorithm" is an important component — it can help AI systems understand the impact of temporal factors on health, thereby providing more precise health advice. The Five Motions and Six Qi algorithm model proposed in this book can serve as an alternative "chronomedicine algorithm" in AI systems. Even if it is ultimately proven not to be the optimal solution, it provides a valuable reference for the algorithm design of AI systems.

Of course, whether the value of this book can ultimately be realized depends on subsequent verification work. If this algorithm model is falsified by large-sample studies, then its value is limited to "providing a falsified conjecture"; if it is partially confirmed, then its value lies in "opening a path for the microscopic verification of chronomedicine"; if it is largely confirmed, then its value lies in "providing an effective algorithmic foundation for chronomedicine in the AI era."

Regardless of the outcome, the author believes that: **In this era of highly developed technology, re-examining the ancient chronomedicine theory of Five Motions and Six Qi, and attempting to verify, correct, and improve it using modern scientific methods, is a meaningful endeavor.** Even if it is ultimately proven wrong, the process of examination itself will deepen our understanding of the relationship between human health and heaven-earth nature.

As the ancients said: "The road is long and far-reaching; I will search up and down." The microscopic verification of chronomedicine is a long and difficult road. This book is merely a starting point on this road, a crude and imperfect starting point. But as long as the direction is correct, no matter how low the starting point, there is always the possibility of reaching the destination.
